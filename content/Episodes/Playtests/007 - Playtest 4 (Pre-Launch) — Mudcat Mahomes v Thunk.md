A catfish-man in a trench coat and a finger-collecting goliath were thrown into **[[The Isolated Keep]]**, the Grandmaster's secret arena, which is stowed away from time and space and riddled with portals. The fight did not stay one-on-one for long. Thanks to a time portal, [[Mudcat Mahomes]] ended up fighting three different versions of [[Thunk]], one of them with eight arms, while dodging a pet dinosaur and a blood elemental named Hamster. It is also the match where a champion shot the Grandmaster. It ended with nobody dead who wasn't already dead, and the two survivors arranging to watch *Top Gun* together.

## The Story

### Before the Fight

The evening opened with [[Grandmaster Elusia Skypiercer|the Grandmaster]] in a poor mood about the last show. [[The Arbiter]]'s previous entertainment, she complained, "included a pig, a spider, and a small little boy who really did not belong here at all." The boy had cried the whole time. She added that she had not known pigs had "that particular power." The Arbiter was unmoved: "They bled, didn't they?" Tonight, he promised, he had brought "fine specimen… of nice breeding stock even." The Grandmaster pointed out that she had no breeding facility, because she'd "already invested a lot in the arena."

Then the [[Champions]] arrived. Thunk, a goliath monk, explained that he had been picking berries on his mountain before he was taken, and that he spent a lot of time up there destroying things. He wore a collection of necklaces made from the fingers of everyone he had killed, and he had more than he could count, "because Thunk can't [count] very well." The Grandmaster immediately wanted a necklace like that.

Mudcat Mahomes was a catfish-man in a trench coat with nothing underneath and a "m'lady fedora." He had one goal: "I guess I'm going to have to kick somebody's ass to get back to Kansas City, because I got some subpar barbecue to eat." He sized up the Arbiter's stripy shirt, decided he looked like a roller-derby referee, and asked whether he was the Grandmaster's best friend. The Arbiter said yes. The Grandmaster corrected him at once: "I don't pay my best friends. He is not a friend. You are employed by me." The Arbiter replied that he'd never filled out a W-2 and that she didn't understand "the complexities of our relationship."

### Into the Isolated Keep

The arena was a circular platform ringed by walls about fifteen feet high, with a huge stone table in the middle and four portals that, the Arbiter said, granted "boons and bonuses, depending how lucky you are." He warned that "there's more than one thing that may bite," and offered a proverb of his own: "You can lead a horse to a portal, but you can't make him travel through interdimensional space." He mentioned that he was writing his memoirs.

The Arbiter led the people's traditional anthem, taking the second verse "down a minor third." The Grandmaster then flew up to an ornate chair on a high perch. [[The Acolytes]] filled a ring of bleachers, "getting wasted and slamming dollar hot dogs," and she gave the champions her terms:

> **The Grandmaster:** "Give me a fight. I want bones broken and I want blood spilt. If not, I shall keep you both here forever."

### First Blood, and the Grandmaster's Arm

Mudcat began with good manners. He folded his trench coat neatly "like daddy told me," set his hat on top, and strung his bow, standing in his catfish birthday suit. His first magic arrow went badly wrong, but the next two hit Thunk. He then studied the time portal, a swirl of stars, clocks spinning both ways, and worlds being made and unmade.

The portals had a catch. Stepping into one seemed to do nothing, but you came out of a random portal and felt *its* effect as you left. The fire portal burned, the blood portal (its floor wriggling with bodies made of blood) withered the flesh, the green nature portal healed, and the time portal scrambled time. "If you thought you could escape my map that easily," the Grandmaster said, "you're a fool."

The Arbiter mocked the name "Mudcat" and called him "an edgelord." The Grandmaster, though, respected a man who folds his clothes. Mudcat protested that the name wasn't about being a catfish: "I think I'm nimble like a cat." Then he called the Grandmaster "Toots," and the Arbiter reminded him that "this is an arena of death and inclusivity."

Thunk flexed the arrows out of his skin ("Thunk never miss leg day"), charged, and punched Mudcat in the gut hard enough to crack ribs. Mudcat replied by conjuring a floating "L" in Thunk's face and firing a weakening arrow. The Grandmaster rewound time so Thunk could try to catch it again, and this time he plucked it perfectly from the air. Except he didn't, because **Mudcat curved the arrow around Thunk and into the Grandmaster's arm.** She pulled it out and snapped it, unharmed: "Your little sticks don't hurt me, nor do your words… You're like a little mouse stuck in a cage here." Mudcat said he'd eaten a mouse before and found it overrated. So she had the Arbiter float a giant rat in front of him and ordered, "Eat the rat." He swallowed it whole like a pelican, and it scratched all the way down.

### Portals and Punches

Thunk was outraged on the Grandmaster's behalf: "You should not have shot at lady." Whole sections of the crowd swooned and began chanting "Thunk!" Then he slipped on the wet floor, twice, and Mudcat conjured a dunce cap with an L on it. When Mudcat broke into air guitar, the Grandmaster ("I am a fan of musicians") blessed him for a round, and the chant switched to "Mudcat." He walked through the green portal to heal, climbed a rock, and loosed six arrows into the fallen goliath in a single burst.

He then tried to win her over properly, shouting "This is what you wanted! I gave you what you wanted!" She was not convinced: "you're coming up a little lacking as a person." Thunk drank the rain, sprang up with a 42-inch vertical leap, and rained punches between Mudcat's legs. The Grandmaster demanded to see how well Mudcat's groin held up. Thunk couldn't find anything to grab, because "they were too small."

A soft voice then sang from all four portals at once. It entranced both fighters and drew them into the nature portal, and both came out of the fire portal burning. Mudcat used his fish slime to fling himself onto the wall and shot Thunk down from there. **Thunk bled out** from what looked like forty-five arrow holes.

### Two-for-One on Fish Night

Then a chill wind blew, and the time portal's clocks stopped and ran backwards. Out walked a past Mudcat Mahomes and a younger Thunk. "I brought you to the Isolated Keep because I can bring as many versions of you back as I want," the Grandmaster explained. The Arbiter was delighted: "We don't bow to the constraints of time. And so now, Mudcat Mahomes, it's a two-for-one special on fish night!" Everyone took a popcorn break. The Grandmaster spat her kernels over the side, noting that the [[Goblins]] would pick them up.

Mudcat banished his past self with obvious satisfaction ("I've been waiting to do this my whole life") and shot young Thunk dead. The Grandmaster was furious that a "small child" had been brought in and promised to put it in the Arbiter's quarterly review. Mudcat was too busy celebrating to notice: "That's right, bitches! You're gonna need a bigger boat!"

### Octothunk

The Grandmaster wanted a better Thunk. Specifically, she wanted "Thunk, just from a different timeline… I want Thunk the Octopus." **Octothunk** arrived with eight arms and twice the punches. He hurled the giant stone table at Mudcat and knocked holes in the Grandmaster's new wall ("I just put it up last month… I paid for some really good contractors"). She announced she would bill Thunk for it. Mudcat mooned him.

The past Mudcat then returned from banishment and drew on Thunk. He couldn't bring himself to shoot. Instead he snapped his bow over his knee and jumped back into the time portal. Whatever happened to him in that banishment, the verdict from ringside was clear: "That boy needs therapy."

### Monsters from the Portals

Next the portals produced monsters: a dryad from the green portal, a fire snake from the fire portal, and a blood elemental from the blood portal. The Grandmaster named the elemental "Hamster" ("I've been wanting to name something hamster for a while"), or Stir for short, and promised the Arbiter a Christmas bonus. Hamster went straight for the dryad.

Mudcat proposed a truce: "Let's team up. We can make a truce. I don't have to kill you a third time." Thunk agreed, while privately planning to betray him. Mudcat shot the fire snake through the eye, sending it back into its portal, and then hit Hamster, who turned to stare at him with tiny black hamster eyes. He tried a triple Lindy over the wall and was flung straight back through the time portal, since everyone knows you can't jump out of the tower. Octothunk then broke the truce with eight-armed punches to the same tender place as before. A small, loyal section of the crowd pelted Thunk with crushed beer cans as "medals."

When Mudcat taunted her, the Grandmaster revealed she had been "saving a little pet of mine." Her dinosaur, **Helena**, clicking and full of sharp carnivore teeth, came through the time portal, bit Mudcat, and dragged him into it before spitting him out elsewhere. Meanwhile Hamster drained the dryad to dust. When Hamster was about to take a free swing at Thunk, the Grandmaster stopped it, scoffing at the idea of rules: "In my game, in my arena, it's my rules and whatever they feel like at that time."

### The After-Party

Then she made an offer. If both survived to the end of the eighth round, she would spare them and let them return to "your picking of berries and little pond." Hamster took the shape of a blood hamster and slammed Thunk, and Mudcat banished it, then dabbed. The Grandmaster told him not to dab. The crowd loved it, and also thought "Hamster" was a stupid name. Octothunk then dabbed with all eight arms, so the Grandmaster removed six of them: "Don't you ever dab here again."

By the end the two survivors had gone soft on each other. "You've killed me before," Thunk said. "You deserve to go home." Mudcat invited him back to his three-level townhome with a two-car garage and an easy chair, promising a thirty-pack of Budweiser and a choice of *Top Gun* or *The Program* unedited on VHS. Thunk would bring Old English, and there would be pork burgers and maybe beer mac and cheese.

The Grandmaster froze time. Moved by "this budding romance," she declared that "as my heart grows a little less cold… I have chosen in the kindness of my heart to spare you both," on one condition: that she be invited to the after-party. She would bring the beer. **She ordered the Arbiter to enter both in the ledger as the night's winners.** The Arbiter agreed to "just pretend that those other Thunks never existed and had no families." Octothunk would be sent home to his octopus world and get his arms back eventually. For now, he was enjoying the novelty: "I've never just had two arms before. This is fun."

- The time portal turned a duel into a crowd: a past Mudcat, a young Thunk, and the eight-armed Octothunk.
- Past Mudcat drew on Thunk, couldn't shoot, broke his own bow and fled back into time.
- The Grandmaster named a blood elemental "Hamster" ("or Stir for short") and summoned her pet dinosaur, Helena.

- The match ended in a shared victory and an after-party invitation the Grandmaster insisted on joining.
