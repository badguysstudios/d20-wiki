The penultimate chapter of the Fate of the Faithful crossover saw [[Lyrus]] the werebear and the travellers from [[Yord]], [[Asena]] and [[John Carmichael]], go hunting for [[Ralzamon]] in the darkest corner of [[The Void]]. There was no arena and no Deathmatch. Instead there was a heist on a floating fortress, a goliath turned into a living bridge, a tiny cat-sized brawl on the battlements, and a duel to the death that ended in a cliffhanger. As Asena put it once the dust settled: "This isn't even the fight!"

## The Story

### How Do We Get Home?

The heroes had just escaped [[The Tumult|the tumult]], a place built from the body of an astral titan and oozing ectoplasmic goo, with a magical silver ewer that could reach the ethereal plane. They had left a fake in its place and found their way back to the Faithful's home base through [[Kenny]]'s secret passages. Back among friends, the question was how John and Asena would ever get back to Yord.

Lyrus had already pointed out that portals here worked differently: "It's an A to B situation." Then [[Lawrence Booker Green]], archivist of the multiverse, snapped his book shut with bad news. The Void sat "outside the bigger picture." The usual in-between planes existed here only in scraps that realms had dragged in with them.

The party talked through options. They could ask [[The Arbiter]] politely, or bet against him, since Lyrus admitted he was "a little bit of a gambler," though whether he kept his bets was another matter. They could trick him, though Lyrus warned, "He's a man who faked his own kidnapping once." Lyrus had his own reason for avoiding pleasantries:

> "We are talking about the man who killed my father, so I'm not really inclined to deal with him politely." — Lyrus

Lawrence then came up with a theory. [[Champions]] who fight in the Void are normally sent home by [[Grandmaster Elusia Skypiercer|the Grandmaster]]. Ralzamon, who had once fought in the kitchen of [[The Dread Fortress]] and was memorably lactose intolerant, had been kept by the Arbiter instead. Lawrence believed the Arbiter had fused that summoned version with the original, snatched from Yord at the moment of his defeat by Asena and John. If they could pull out the echo that still belonged to Yord, its tie to home might lead them back. Lawrence suspected the Arbiter had been harvesting fallen champions, and Lyrus added the darker part: the Arbiter had been amassing an army.

### The Seer's Divination

Lawrence asked about the stronghold beyond [[The Eastern Outpost|the eastern outpost]], which the Faithful had once stormed back when they thought the Arbiter had been kidnapped. Lyrus suggested somewhere worse: [[The Shadowlands]], a largely unmapped realm of darkness and creatures of the night, said to hold a spiked central spire. For sympathetic links, John brought out a ceremonial chalice taken from an occult chamber. Asena dug out a sepia photograph of her family's 1930s expedition party, Josiah Parsons (Ralzamon's original name) among them, folded among gum wrappers and a poisonous mushroom.

[[The Seer]], found admiring the artwork, agreed to help. He admitted that "the one you speak of has certainly clouded my vision as of late." While the ritual ran, John kept morale up by reciting King Théoden's speech from *The Return of the King*. He did it in full hologram, riding on Lyrus's shoulders with a projected horse wearing Lyrus's face. He kept falling off. The verdict: the speech was inspiring, but John was not.

Lawrence came out of the ritual bleeding from one ear. The Seer was completely fine and baffled: "It's just a bit of divination. I don't understand what the big deal is." Lawrence described the vision as "like trying to find a dark pebble in a puddle of oil." Ralzamon was in a stronghold floating near the shadow of the central spire, "isolated by a rift," with something moving in the depths below. The Dread Fortress was the sun and the spire was the sundial.

### Preparations

Lyrus handed out wooden stakes "for the Vampiric Kobolds" and refused to explain beyond "They have pack tactics, just so you know." Asena received a potion of force resistance, with only the advice that it would come in handy if she started hitting herself. A former associate of Ralzamon's, the Hextech Master, opened with a mocking impression of the "master of things other people are unaware of." The Seer then presented John with a rainbow-sheened Damascus knife he had forged for John's cat, Chester, who chose to hold it in his mouth.

Asena found Lawrence among his books. He offered his philosophy: "If it's confusing, you can just punch it." He would stay behind as their only lifeline if things went wrong. As a divination anchor he wanted her blood, and he produced a vintage bloodletting set suspiciously quickly. Asena cut her palm "like they do in the movies," because "The blood sounds way cooler."

Lyrus explained his heritage: "I'm actually a Goliath. Just happen to be a werebear as well." His father, [[Orin]], had been turned as a child in the same attack the Arbiter saved him from. Lyrus kept the bear form in his memory. Then he stabbed both swords into the air, ripped open a blind portal, and landed it on the first try.

### Into the Shadowlands

On the other side, a freezing gale clawed at their fur and light seemed to die before it could cross the air. The wind carried whispers and screams. Ahead stood a gatehouse with a massive portcullis, and beyond it a bridge over a violet-lit, bottomless moat to a Gothic keep on a floating island. Far below, something enormous slithered through the air "with far, far too many legs." At the gate, [[Goblins]] in chef's hats, some marked with vampiric kobold bites, fought over scraps.

The Shadowlands then reached into their minds. John saw shadows creep up the trees like regenerating flesh and heard the trees whisper in a language he shouldn't understand. Lyrus saw the whole place built from his father's bones and growled, "Get out of my head." Asena alone saw Josiah Parsons standing at the gate while a voice warned, "He cannot know… If he does not know, he can take no vengeance." [[Circe]], the party's magitech master, scanned with her goggles and declared, "The data and the evidence weren't aligned, but now they are, so we're fine."

### The Heist

A goblin grabbed a severed arm and was disintegrated by a red ray from the walls. John tested it with an illusory goblin, which was also vaporized, and so concluded the test of the Goblin Disintegration System. The sentries couldn't see through illusions. John wrapped the party in a moving camouflage dome, and Circe shrank everyone to cat size so they could squeeze through the portcullis. Circe got stuck. Twice.

Inside waited the fortress's defenders: rows of half-machine, half-flesh converted demons and a hulking upgraded demon with a blaster arm and rocket pack ("It's not a cyberdemon, but that's a goddamn cyberdemon"). Before them floated a mind witness, a beholder twisted into a telepathic sentry. The bridge was broken, so Lyrus proposed the obvious solution: Circe made him enormous, and he became the bridge. "Just going up Mount Lyrus here," said John. "Careful, that tickles… just watch out for the sensitive areas," said Lyrus.

When John spoke telepathically, every eye on the mind witness swung toward him. He sacrificed a decoy, Weebo, a little sentient machine from his briefcase. The mind witness carried it over the abyss and dropped it. "No, Weebo!" Giant Lyrus bumped the mind witness with his thigh and hopped past on one foot, shaking the courtyard. A sign from John reading "come here, come here, come here" then froze the mind witness in place. Lyrus failed to sneak up the stairs, the guards went on alert, and he stepped over the inner wall to find himself eye to eye with the lord of the fortress.

### The Duel

It was a Draconic Death Knight, the rotting, reanimated corpse of a dragonborn knight in unholy armor. Lyrus tried a bluff: "We were sent by the Arbiter to check on the, you know, the thing about the thing… You know how he is." It failed.

Lyrus hurled tiny John across the keep. John tore a tattoo from his leg, which became dragon wings, and unleashed a wave of fear on everyone in the keep. One panicked mercenary blew a horn carved from an undead dragon's horn, sounding the alarm. The watching Acolytes declared it time to duel, so nobody else could interfere. Six barrels of corrosive fluid also appeared "for no adequately explained reason." An Acolyte named Jack then bankrolled Lyrus, who spent the gold on extra fury.

Lyrus crushed down through the knight's shield with green-flamed, holy strikes. The knight parried again and again and struck back. John threw a magical shield over the bear to turn the blows aside. Thirty skeletons and a giant bone ballista fired at John, and once his shield went up, everything bounced off. Aulus the Kindler, the man who had claimed the fake ewer, tried to call down sunlight, found John out of range, and hurled a fireball instead. John whooshed straight through the blast: "Your measly human weapons cannot harm me, for I am John Carmichael, destroyer of worlds and annihilator of your stupid keep." He went on to carve into the candle-headed Candle Jack.

Asena, who had dived between the bars of the inner portcullis and gotten stuck too ("Why does everyone in our group have dump truck butts?" John asked), slipped free and wrote to Circe with her distance quill, begging to be made big. The message appeared on Circe's goggle lenses ("what's this crap on my lenses?"). Meanwhile Asena dropped onto a mercenary like a devil squirrel, wrapped her legs around his neck and punched him until he walked off the edge and splattered on the ground below.

Then Lyrus landed the blow the knight couldn't parry, and the knight fell. His undead soul rose from his eyes as a pulsing orb of green and black hellfire. Lyrus leapt fifty feet off the tower onto a skeleton, crushing it instantly, and shrugged off a swarm of arrows. Then the orb hurled itself at its slayer and exploded, engulfing the courtyard, cooking the skeletons into glass and swallowing the towering werebear. All of this, the party learned, was only the prelude to Ralzamon himself.

### Lore statements
- **The Void has no direct connection to normal cosmology.** Fragments of ethereal, astral and shadow planes cling to the realms that were brought into it (Lawrence's theory).
- **Champions are returned home by the Grandmaster. The Arbiter handles losers, and has kept echoes of fallen champions and "harvested" them, fusing them with their originals at the moment of death** (Lawrence's theory, supported by Lyrus).
- **The Void has things "that would rival the early Titans"** (stated by Mudcat as Lyrus's player). Their portents make trees come alive and watch.
- The Shadowlands were the Shadow King's realm, and the Arbiter draws shadow creatures from them.
