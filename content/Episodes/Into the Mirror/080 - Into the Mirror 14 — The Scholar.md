With the dragon dead and its treasure cave theirs for the taking, **[[Flynry]]**, **[[Toddleheim]]**, **[[Elianna]]** and **[[Cerys]]** went looking for the one prize that mattered: a golden orrery. They found it, and then it found Flynry's sword. The meeting threw them into an unknown corner of the desert and down into a half-buried library, where a giant winged man was pulling books off the shelves only to watch each one crumble. There was no fight this time, only a slow, creeping mystery with a final reveal that landed like a slap.

## The Story

### Spoils of the Dragon

The episode opened in the quiet after the battle with the dragon. Cerys was still giddy about how she had finished it, with what she called "those two nat 20s… back to back," two perfect blows in a row. Where the beast had fallen, it had collapsed into a pile of snow. In that snow Cerys spotted a glint and pulled out a **soul gem about the size of her palm**. It was large, uncut and rough, and it was something none of them had seen before. Every soul gem they had come across "in the other places" had been green. This one was clear white, like a diamond, though it held the same ethereal inner light as the others.

Cerys flashed the "big honking gem" at her companions, declared "This is quite something," and pocketed it. Then she took charge: "Well, let's get a-lookin' for this orrery. I command."

The only other time Flynry had seen Cerys in combat, she had been on her knees and bleeding. Watching her take on a dragon toe to toe, and with such flair, had raised her a good deal in his estimation.

### The Crown Jewel

The hoard was everything a dragon's hoard should be. There were heaps of gold and gems of every size, and they could fill their pockets with as much as they could carry. There was also some ordinary armor, none of it worth swapping for. At the back of the cave, perched on the tallest pile of gold as if on a pedestal, sat **the crown jewel of the collection: a golden orrery**. It was a gyroscope of interwoven rings around a central orb, about half the size of a basketball. It gave off such a presence that you didn't need to be a wizard to know it was magical.

"You two might want to come up here and have a look at this," Flynry called. Cerys agreed: "This might be our winner, our item that we're looking for." Flynry, however, had no intention of laying a finger on it.

> **Flynry:** "I might have seen it, but I'm not going to touch it. We all know what the king looked like."

Elianna wanted to be careful too. She noted that the dragon "didn't look like it had any wounds on it from before," then snapped her fingers and sent a wave of white light rolling across the cave to outline anything dangerous. Danger would have shown up red, and nothing did. There were no traps.

Flynry pointed out that Toddleheim was the wizard. Toddleheim answered, "Well, I guess I'll take one for the team," then added modestly that Elianna was "doing a fantastic job." He bounded up the gold in his werewolf form, leapt onto the ledge, shifted back into himself as he landed, and picked up the orrery.

Up close it was hefty and lifeless. It was shiny gold flecked with green, copper-like patina. Its rings spun by hand, and markings ran around each one, including symbols that looked like numbers. Toddleheim knew five languages and recognized none of them. Flynry hopefully offered Elvish, but it wasn't Elvish. It was **Celestial**, which Elianna could read. Toddleheim hopped down and handed it to her. The faded inscription struck her as oddly familiar and specific, enough to take her aback:

> "The path to dreams begins with the truth."

### Sword Meets Orrery

Explaining the words as she went, Elianna dashed toward Flynry's sword. As she drew near, **the orrery's rings began spinning in different directions**. It lifted out of her hands and hovered, whirring mechanically, and Flynry's sword began to glow the color of its flames without igniting. The closer she came, the faster the rings spun. The two objects pulled at each other like magnets, and the sword rose slightly to meet the orrery. When metal scraped metal, **a flash of light blinded them all**.

### The Green Desert

When their eyes cleared, they were once again in a desert of green. It was part of [[The Sands of Eternity]], but a place none of them had ever been. The sky and the sand looked familiar. The building in front of them did not. It was enormous and very old, made of costly stone and marble, with ornate columns and carved gargoyles. It was **tilted at nearly 45 degrees**, its bottom corner sunk into the sand, and lights glowed inside. The orrery had fallen lifeless back into Elianna's hands, and the sword had gone dark.

Elianna asked Cerys whether she had been here before. She hadn't, and Flynry was uneasy:

> **Flynry:** "This is different from where I woke up. I've never seen that structure before. Something here feels… off."

Cerys wondered whether they could get back: "If we say the name of the city, maybe it'll work. I don't know." Flynry admitted they were in uncharted territory. Cerys reasoned that "that orrery brought us here for a reason." Flynry settled it: "I haven't had a reason to doubt my sword yet. Well, let's go then."

### The Sunken Library

Sand spilled down the half-buried entry steps into **a library sunk in the middle of the desert**. Every shelf was full, candles and lanterns burned everywhere, and loose pages lay strewn across the desks. Over the hiss of falling sand came a voice, muttering on an endless loop: "I don't know what's happening… I need to know, I need to know." And then: "I need to know all of this knowledge."

Behind a bookshelf they found the source. He was a man seven or eight feet tall, with **great black wings** down his back, his hair pulled back with the sides shaved close, and a large mustache. He wore leathers and robes of blue and black. He pulled book after book from the shelves, and **each one crumbled to sand** in his hands. He was in a dismayed trance, trapped in a loop.

Flynry recognized it. When he had first arrived in this realm, he had woken into something like a dream, a trance, maybe some sort of hell. "Something about the way he is acting reminds me of my first arrival here," he said. So he sheathed his sword on his back and approached with open hands. "My friend, greetings." The man ignored him: "I have to know." Flynry answered gently, "I too would like to know," and laid a hand on his shoulder.

### The Face of the Scholar

At Flynry's touch, light came back into the man's eyes. He reached for one more book, stopped, glanced sideways at Flynry and shook his head. His desperation melted into joy, and his confidence flooded back as if he had lost himself for a moment and only now found the way home. Then the joy curdled into something smug, a wry smile with an air of self-importance and more than a little arrogance.

"This is excellent," he said. "I know exactly what I need to do now. I know my path." He gripped Flynry by the shoulder: "You can be useful to me." Then, to all of them: "I can make good use of all of you. You will do nicely."

He looked younger than they remembered, and his hair and mustache were different. But there was no mistaking him. **This was a face they had all seen before: the face of [[The Arbiter]].** Elianna managed only "Oh!" and Cerys stammered, "No! You know I… Oh…" The episode ended on that.
