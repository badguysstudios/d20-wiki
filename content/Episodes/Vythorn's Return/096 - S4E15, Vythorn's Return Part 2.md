There was no Deathmatch this week and no grandstand full of Acolytes. Instead, [[The Arbiter|Vythorn, the Arbiter]] woke in the silent forest of [[Fil]], the God of Fate, and had to walk to the tome he had been chasing: the Testaments of [[Morir's Cataclysm]]. Fil gave him two companions who had already failed her tests, the shapeshifter [[Ise]] and the tiny, extremely loud grung [[Biff]]. The road brought a lost child, a cave full of gnolls, a door that drinks blood and a hourglass that only runs one way. By the end Vythorn had his book, a new reason to hate [[The Shadow King|the Shadow King]], and a vow that would shake [[The Void]].

## The Story

### Waking in the Silent Forest

The dream faded and Vythorn found himself on his back in the mud, with Fil leaning over him, her chestnut hair hanging down. "Are you awake yet?" She welcomed him to her "lovely little home," a forest. She pointed out that forests were something the Void didn't really have, with all its volcano, ash and field. A muddy caravan road ran through the trees. The woods were as dead quiet as the swamp he had just left: no birds, no snakes, not even a bug.

The Testaments, Fil explained, lay an hour or two down the road in the Ruins of Giant's Breath. She would not carry him there, and she would not simply hand the book over. "If you want to be the God of Fate," she told him, "you need to have your own hands in play."

### Two Companions and an Egg Timer

With a wave of both hands, the dirt on either side of the road rose and took shape as two people. Ise and Biff had been pulled out of time and space just before their final breaths and restored to their prime. They would be Fil's eyes and ears, and at the end of the walk she would ask them whether Vythorn deserved what he came for.

Neither was thrilled. Ise wanted to know what happened if he refused to dance on her strings for free. Fil asked sweetly whether he missed being dirt, "because I can step on you again." Biff conceded that was a pretty good argument. Fil promised candy, which won Biff over completely. Ise does not like candy. He looked Vythorn up and down and gave his verdict:

> **Ise:** "He won't make it. Send us back now."

Fil agreed that Vythorn looked disappointing. She mentioned she had "almost let him drown earlier" and kept him only for entertainment. She handed Biff a small, heavy hourglass, about two hours of sand, "just enough time for you to get to the ruins," and vanished with a snap, muttering about the bets going on up above. The sand kept falling the same way however Biff turned it. He called it "a pretty poorly made egg timer," and Ise had no idea what an egg timer was.

On the road, Vythorn introduced himself as "right hand to [[Grandmaster Elusia Skypiercer|the Grandmaster]] of the Void, or Arbiter." Ise was unimpressed. He had already lifted Vythorn's coin purse, and he tossed it back. Biff pulled a spiked glaive more than twice his own height out of a backpack stuffed with junk. Ise turned himself into a grung to teach Biff to walk quietly, because Biff's flat feet slapped the mud "like slapping pancakes onto another freaking flapjack." Biff tried it, caught his foot on a sharp rock and yelped. Vythorn's eyes went a milky, voidy white, multicolored lightning crackled around him, and time slipped back ten seconds so that Biff never tripped. "Well, I'm something of a scholar," Vythorn said. Biff decided that was "a book-learning thing."

Vythorn also tried his time magic on the hourglass. The sand froze and trembled, pulled both ways, and then kept falling. Here, at least, Fil's rules held.

### The Carriage in the Woods

Through the fog they came upon a carriage with its door hanging open and two horses standing perfectly still. Ise, now disguised as Vythorn's squire, said drily that he was sure an abandoned carriage in a haunted wood was exactly what it seemed. Biff said outright it might be a trap. Vythorn chose to risk it. Ise and Biff both heard a whimper inside. Ise put a finger to his lips, and the pair let Vythorn walk straight in.

While Vythorn dug through the books inside, hoping one was the Testaments, a small shape dropped onto his back and drove a dagger into his neck. The attacker was **Kuro**, a frail tabaxi child with fluffy blonde hair. Ise instantly turned into a matching tabaxi, and the terrified boy dropped his blade. Vythorn threatened to "extract" the child's name until Kuro gave it. Kuro said he and his mother had been travelling to the academy when they heard a small voice in the woods. His mother went to look, "because she's very brave," and then he heard her scream.

Ise healed Vythorn's neck by licking it with a rough cat's tongue. Vythorn's reply: "If you ever do that again, I will kill you." Ise: "Unlikely, but okay." Biff gave Kuro a hard roll of bread from his pack. Then Vythorn made his choice: "I suppose if I'm going to be a God of Fate, I should get used to intervening. Or not intervening." He set off after the mother with Ise. Biff stayed with the boy ("Stick with me, kid. You'll be fine") and "barricaded" the carriage with books, backpacks, a dead tree and something from his pack that looked like it should be dead. None of it would stop anything, but it calmed Kuro.

### The Gnoll Cave

Vythorn proved hopeless at tracking. "I'm more of the book type. I've read about many forests," he said. Ise eventually nudged him toward a bush where one set of footprints became many, with claw marks and drag lines. The trail led to a brightly lit cave. Inside was a settled gnoll camp with families, cooking fires and pups. At the far end, the tabaxi mother sat in a wooden pen under a shaft of light.

Ise slipped ahead as a drow. Vythorn turned himself invisible and followed without telling him. Barefoot, because he had lost his Italian leather boots in the swamp, he walked straight through the middle of camp. One gnoll caught the scent of "something that smells like it's bathed recently." Vythorn rewound time to dodge it, and the déjà vu made things worse. "Something is here," the gnoll growled, and the hunt was on. Vythorn laid a false trail with an illusion. The only footprints he could picture were Biff's, so phantom pancake-prints went slapping across the cave floor, and the gnolls pounced on them shouting "Ghost here!"

Biff had arrived by then, dragging his feet to keep them quiet, and summoned his Echo: his shadow peeled off the wall and became a second, void-dark Biff. "Somehow I feel like I've made this worse."

Ise had taken the exact form of the gnoll leader, down to the sagging right shoulder and the missing toe. He called a shadow blade behind the real leader, invoked their god ("Yenogu won't smile upon your treachery") and killed him with one blow. Then he kicked the body over and announced, "Brothers and sisters, you've been deceived by a shapeshifter." It almost worked, except the real leader always ended his charges with a howl, and Ise never howled. "My primary export is violence," he explained, and the swirling bladesong that followed looked nothing like the old leader.

Vythorn dropped his invisibility and slowed half a dozen gnolls. Biff charged with his glaive, screaming the battle cry of the grung, then spat poison into a gnoll's eyes and turned to the rest: "I've got a glaive!" The gnolls panicked ("It's got a glaive!") and became convinced the legendary cave frogs had come to reclaim their caves. When the cage guard drove a spear at the mother, Biff's Echo teleported in front of the bars, took the blow and flipped the gnoll off. Ise offered the pack one more chance: "I offered you mercy. Now burn." He threw a fireball into the crowd and caught Vythorn in it. "You might say fate intervened." He told the survivors to take the cubs and flee, and some did. Vythorn put an orb of chaos lightning into a gnoll's mouth and blew out the back of its head. Biff saw off the last one: "You've seen the hard way. I'd run!"

### The Mother and the Shortcut

The cage was only tied shut. The mother's foot was mangled, and when Ise turned into Kuro to offer healing, she saw through it and grabbed a stake to fend him off. Vythorn, watching the clock, simply froze her with a spell. The hourglass was more than three-quarters gone. Biff, holding it upside down, insisted no time had passed. Ise asked Biff, the group's self-appointed moralist, whether he should heal her against her wishes. Biff said yes, and Ise did it, adding helpfully that if she was still mad later, he'd "be more than willing to chop it off."

Kuro had left the carriage. When he clung to Vythorn's knee, Vythorn was ready to punch the child out of his way, and Biff leapt in to pull Kuro clear. "One of us has to be a little less selfish than the rest," Biff said, and brought the boy to his mother. She turned out to be a guide who knew the forest's magical shortcuts. Her hand glowed yellow, red, then green, and a glowing path opened toward the Ruins of Giant's Breath. Biff ran down Vythorn to show him: "Like I said, you always have time." Vythorn asked why she hadn't just said so. Biff answered, "Why didn't you just ask or stop to help or consider anything other than yourself for more than a minute?" Vythorn only replied, "And when I'm gone, you'll still be here."

### Breaking the Hourglass

In a cramped tunnel, the companions confessed how they had failed. Ise had spared a defeated opponent who begged for mercy, and was wrong to. Biff had sacrificed himself so his grung companions could reach their prize, and they all failed anyway. Vythorn smiled wryly: he thought he understood the test now. Biff's line kept nagging at him, *you always have time*, so he took the hourglass and looked closer. It had been mended "time and time again," held together only by binding magic. Vythorn dispelled it, and the sand ran through his fingers.

"Well, at least the time is in my own hands now." (Biff pointed out that this was very funny, because his hands were covered in sand.) Along the way the party also took a detour into the anatomy of eggs, and Biff learned he has a cloaca. He walked the rest of the tunnel staring at himself and screaming, but he came out a little smarter.

### The Thirsty Door

The tunnel opened onto a ravine of giant bones and a door ringed with giant skulls. "Kind of reminds me of home," said Vythorn. Carved into the door was a giant's gaping mouth like a bowl, and an inscription in Giant that Biff could read, or at least the big letters: "I am thirsty." Water vanished into it, and the carved eyes woke. Ise and Biff asked Vythorn, "How badly do you want this?"

"More than you know." Vythorn stabbed Ise and pressed him against the mouth to bleed. Ise held his gaze the whole time and told him he stabbed like a scholar. When the bowl still wasn't full, Ise took Vythorn's hand and pushed the knife deeper himself: "The difference between you and me is I understand what I owe." The eyes flashed red and the door sank into the ground. Biff propped up the pale, bleeding Ise as a crutch.

### The Scribe of Morir

Inside, an old aarakocra with faded feathers, a monocle and metal claws for hands woke with a start: "Oh, good, you're finally here. I've been waiting forever." This was [[Master Skreca]], keeper, janitor and cook of a blue-lit museum of tomes and curiosities. Its wares included a set of armor that does nothing except let you sleep in it. The rule was strict: one item, the blood price, or nobody leaves. That also put an end to Biff's faint hope of finding his lost companions there.

Skreca told the story. Morir had been a humble man who loved everything, even the endlessly warring giants. When the giants killed a captive prince in their bombardment, Morir drew power from the spilled blood and spoke unholy words. "He split us from the world itself," and "a million teeth of demons began to devour their flesh." Skreca had been his scribe and wrote the Testaments down. "It's the testaments themselves in which I lost my hands." Asked why he wanted such a thing, Vythorn answered: "I have a terrible foe that must die. So that my home may live."

### Faces from the Past

Ise shed his disguises for his true form, a tiny, ashen-white changeling. He asked the foe's name. "His name is Agdrel. He is called the Shadow King." Ise smiled: "That's why I know you. As you might imagine, I never forget a face." He became Vythorn's father, then his mother. Long ago, working for "less than reputable parties," he had watched the Shadow King take their lives with his own hands.

Vythorn had spent his whole life believing his own uncontrolled chaos had killed them. Wearing his mother's face, Ise asked whether the truth made him want to defeat the Shadow King more or less. Vythorn's answer was "Son of a bitch!"

### Judgment

Fil stepped out from behind a column. Ise asked to be her instrument instead of returning to the dust. She accepted. His blood twisted into strange characters across his body, and he became a small crow, her pet, who would change when she wanted him to change. His verdict on Vythorn: grant him the tome and "let him end up where he seems intent to go."

Biff turned down the candy and the collar. "I would rather die on my terms as a free grung than live as a pet in any capacity." Those moments had been his, and his sacrifice was his fate. (He might have reconsidered for lemon drops. There were none.) Fil, sorry to have less to bet on, granted him eternal sleep.

Then she knelt to Vythorn's eye level, and he made his vow: "I am a demon. Born of lies, shaped and molded by chaos… I will return home. And I will pull the Shadow King's strings until he unravels into nothing." After that, he would take fate into his own hands, "and my will will be done across the void because I say so." Fil sighed that she should have turned him into a parrot, but she loved the ego. "I think you're a winning bet until you're not." The Testaments were his, on one condition: "do not disappoint, or I will take it all away. And you will be mine forever." His eyes closed, and he was returned deep into the Void.

## Dried Blood

After the show, the table shared what was going on beneath the surface. Kuro was meant as a small mirror of Vythorn: a young boy with budding magic who could have been left parentless and alone. Vythorn was too focused on the hourglass and himself to notice. The real test, it turned out, was whether he would break the system, because a would-be god of fate doesn't work within other people's boundaries. Skreca's hands were eaten away because the words of the Testaments are so dangerous that even writing them unravels reality. With the truth about his parents, killing the Shadow King has become personal for Vythorn: still selfish, but a different kind of selfish.

- The changeling reveals he saw Agdrel kill Vythorn's parents, undoing the guilt Vythorn had carried all his life.
