There was no arena, no opponent and no crowd this week. **Vythorn**, [[The Arbiter|the Arbiter]] (played by Mudcat), tumbled out of a portal and into a place outside every map he knew: a lifeless swamp, a lake, and a lone tower standing in the in-between. The match was against himself, and his opponent was the patient, laughing, many-faced [[Fil|God of Fate]]. It cost him a pair of very nice boots and won him a quest.

## The Story

### Falling Through Nothing

The tale opened with Vythorn already in motion. He had just slipped away from his companions, opening a portal that left them "hidden still within the mirror." The narration was blunt about it: he had betrayed them, but he held firm to one promise, to return to [[The Void|the Void]] and to Elusia's side.

He fell through space and time inside swirling blue and green auras and felt nothing at all. There was no wind and no breeze, and not even air when he breathed. Each time he reached for the aura, it slowed. The colors faded to white, then to black, until his feet finally touched ground.

### The Black River

The ground squelched. "Your shoes were not meant for this," the narrator observed. "Desert life may have suited them well, but here, it is moist." Vythorn stood in a cool, humid swamp of gnarled trees and dead branches with no life in it at all: no fish, no bugs, not even a gnat. A black, murky river ran past a small, long-unused dock, and at its end sat a little boat with an ornate lantern on chains. He knew without a doubt he had never been here, and he knew he was not in the Void.

The black droplets on his fingers would not come off. When he studied them, he found the substance was alive and faintly charged with magic. In the warmth of his skin and the lantern's light, a dollop swelled to the size of his palm. Inside it, black and glittering gold swirled, and faces and eyes looked out at him before they were swept back in. "Interesting, but I don't really have time for that," he decided, and flung it into the river.

He suspected the light and warmth were stirring the sludge, so he hung the lantern as far from himself as he could, at the front of the boat. Then he tried to row. The Endless Scholar turned out to have no idea how to paddle a boat, so he threw down the oar in frustration and conjured a blast of wind behind the boat. Suddenly it had an engine, and it roared down the river with a booming racket.

### The Fork and the Failing Light

The river split three ways. The right branch was dark, low-hung and foggy. The left and center were brighter and faster, with glimmers ahead like "little lanterns or fireflies or stars." Muttering **"smarter, not harder,"** Vythorn steered left. The river widened until he could see neither bank. There were only fog and soft lights, as though he had sailed into the middle of an ocean.

He was moving too fast. Every splash threw more black ooze onto the lantern until three quarters of its light was gone. When he leaned forward to wipe it clean, the stuff pushed back, almost aggressively, and climbed over the remaining panels. In the last inch of light he saw the murk creeping over the sides of the boat "as if claws and tendrils pull it down."

### The Thing That Ate Magic

His eyes went milky with void-light, lightning crackled at his fingertips, and he fired a bolt of purple electricity to strip away whatever magic was at work. [[The inkslime creature|The substance]] laughed at him, grew a mouth, ate the spell and grew bigger. It left him a single sliver of light, as if to mock him.

"What are you?" he demanded.

> **The inkslime creature:** "What am I? Oh, you should ask who we are. That's the question."

Vythorn reached into the sky and pulled the clouds backward and forward through time, dragging down a storm that had once hung over this place, and called its lightning onto the creature. He felt the familiar magic gather, and then nothing happened. "Oh, they didn't give you permission to do that, did they?" the creature crooned, slithering up his calves. "I don't need your permission," he snapped, and he escaped its grip only by kicking off his Italian leather boots. The creature happily gobbled them up.

"We are trapped, and now you are trapped," it told him, pinning his legs and wrapping his torso as the boat sank level with the river. It offered to take him somewhere, if he was specific. "I need to get back to the Grandmaster. Let me go." When that didn't work, he stabbed where its head should be. His fist went straight through, and it kept laughing: "Silly boy, silly boy… Come with us and you will stay here forever."

Panicking now, he tried to vanish into the ethereal plane. The creature simply appeared inside his mind: "We told you. They didn't give you permission to do that. That's not how it works here." "Then educate me," he said. "Then come with us… You have to just let go."

So he let go. The slime became a warm blanket pulled up to his neck, and gentle fingers brushed his cheeks. He sank into weightless black until the lantern's light found him, dancing in a little infinity symbol: "It's time. Wake up."

### The Lake and the Tower

Vythorn followed the light up through what felt like an ocean and burst into the air, gasping, in a lake. A lone tower stood on a carved-out spit of land, with flickering lights and moving shadows inside. He then discovered that he could not swim. He had read about swimming, surely, but had never done it a day in his life. He thrashed for a few feet before learning the water was only two feet deep, "as if the ground itself has risen just to make you look like a fool." Fortunately, as far as he knew, no one saw.

As he trudged ashore, a small black head with no eyes bobbed up in the water: "If they don't like you, you can always come back to see us." Vythorn answered with all his dignity: "I'll be back to see you. And I'll dance on your grave!" Asked whether he felt good about that, he said he felt great about it.

A snap of his fingers turned back time on his clothes, and they were dry, pressed and spotless. His feet stayed bare. (At the table, a brief debate broke out over his sockless lifestyle. "Good boots don't need socks," he insisted. "Oh, if they're Italian.") A rock then whizzed out of nowhere and hit him in the back of the head, along with a shout of "they gave you permission to do that." He refused to dignify it and walked on with his jaw clenched.

[[The tower in the in-between|The tower]] made him uneasy. Its grass was untrodden, as though no guest had ever come or gone, and seven lanterns just like the one on his boat hung in its windows. He tried to shield himself against otherworldly things and then knocked, in a way the narrator described as "less of a polite knock and more of a just like, open the fucking door." Nobody answered. The door had no handle, and it swung open at a push.

Inside, a massive, vibrant young tree grew up through the bare stone tower, with no stairs anywhere. Vythorn climbed it barefoot, and for once the missing boots helped, because his toes gripped the bark.

### The Gathering

At the top was an enchanting room, with the tree blossoming through it, a warm hearth, a ceiling full of lanterns and books everywhere. Fifteen to twenty people filled it: aarakocra, halflings, elves and stranger folk, reading, chatting and sipping tea. Nobody looked at him, until he asked where he was. Every conversation stopped at once and every eye turned to him.

They answered one at a time and finished one another's thoughts. An elf said, "We were expecting you… It just wasn't your time yet." The whole room laughed together like a choir, each voice on a different note. A mouse-like creature with big white ears said, "Well, we're your way back." A nine-foot woman with purple blossoms brushing her head explained, "We exist between every plane, every time, every space, in between." "Am I still dead?" he asked. A little boy assured him it was "not really a dead place, but it is an end if you want it to be."

At the back sat a woman with chestnut hair and long white wings folded behind her, staring at the floor. "Vythorn, breathe. We've asked you to be here. And you are already eager to leave." A leonin told him, "I know you"; a blonde human man added, "I've known you too"; and a white-furred bear said, "I've known you." Vythorn sneaked a look at the shelves and found that every book, scroll and tome was blank, even the ones being discussed. A cat nudged his heels ("I'm allergic to cats"), and a drink was in his hand before he could refuse it.

### Turning the Pages

They said they were there to help, "in the way that we want." "Ah, I see why you've called yourself the Endless Scholar," the winged woman said. "You ask so many questions and you think you're smarter for it." A tabaxi explained that they had plucked him out of his journey between spaces: "It was so easy. Fishing's harder, actually."

When he introduced himself as "the Arbiter of the Dread Fortress, right hand of the Grandmaster," she waved it away. Those were titles, and all of them he had given himself. She wanted the pages underneath. He took his first sip, which was sweet and warm with cinnamon, "like a comfort of Christmas on a cold day," and finally admitted, "I don't have a home. Is that what you want to hear?"

> **The God of Fate:** "To know where you're going, you need to know exactly where you've been."

The drink pulled him into a dream made of cloud. In it a young boy who looked a lot like him stood lost in the rain, his hands wreathed in magical fire, while a two-story house burned behind him with screams coming from inside. "I suppose my family would say too much potential," Vythorn said. "Dangerous, weren't you?" "Still am." A polar bear snarled in his face, asking who had ever stayed long enough to call family. He answered that he had had teachers and friends, and that for a time even [[The Shadow King|the Shadow King]] himself had been one. "Friends don't put friends in chests," a lizard with an eyeglass reminded him.

The winged woman touched his forehead and took him back to the moment the Shadow King was first struck down in the days of [[Skypiercer]], when he lay dying beneath Vythorn and Vythorn chose not to finish him. "I… couldn't just let his power go to waste," he said. He argued that he had used that power to keep order while the Grandmaster held her games, and that he had brought Elusia home across the planes and into [[The Dread Fortress|the Dread Fortress]]. "She's a great figurehead, very inspirational. Inspiration only goes so far." The woman was unmoved. The Void had run itself for centuries without that power, she said, and the plot to overthrow Elusia "was a seed that was already planted by you." Had he never been dissatisfied with her rule, or her father's? "I don't know, maybe? Sometimes, who's not?"

### What Vythorn Wants

They pressed him. What did he dream of? He didn't know. An angry owlbear flipped the table, scattering the brew. The winged woman's wings opened as she listed what he had done: he kept the Shadow King in a box, sacrificed other people's lives and dreams, and fought on with barely a friend beside him. It couldn't be for loyalty, she said, and it couldn't simply be knowledge for its own sake.

Under his breath he finally said it: **"I want to control fate."**

The room erupted, because it was the funniest thing he could possibly have said. "I will be the God of Fate," he went on over the laughter. "I will decide for everyone." For the first time the winged woman looked him in the eye and asked what choice a God of Fate would make. "The only choice that matters. I'll choose to move the pawns instead of being one." Then, to the giggling room: "It's not funny."

### The Bargain

"Some call us desire, some ambition or providence," said the cat. "Risk, destiny, luck, hope even, fortune, demise, even fate," said the bear. "You see, we are many things. And we are one." Every figure in the room lost its shape and poured into the winged woman, and only the two of them remained. "You want to be us."

"I suppose I want to kill you," Vythorn said. "What was never alive cannot be killed." "Then you won't mind."

She offered him control of the fates of everyone in the Void. It was not every fate in all of time and space, but it was "much of what you desire." "It's a start," he said. If his choices impressed them, he might even become one of them. First he had to find a tome that "notes power of all sorts. Chaos and time and luck." She warned him that if his vision faltered, they would make sure his dream never came true. "My vision has never been clearer," he said, and took her hand.

"Good. Now go find [[Morir's Cataclysm|the Testaments of Morir's Cataclysm]]." She blew on him, a gust swept him backward, and the world went black.

At the table afterward, it was revealed that the fork in the river had never mattered. The left and center branches would have made no difference, and the dark right-hand one simply looped back to offer the choice again. It had never occurred to Vythorn that his choices might not count.
