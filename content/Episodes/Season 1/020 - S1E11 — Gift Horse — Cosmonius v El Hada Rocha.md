Two of the most gorgeous [[Champions|champions]] ever to enter the arena met in the humming heart of [[The Dread Fortress]]: **[[Cosmonius]]**, a Fabio-haired centaur monk played by RobertHartleyGM, and **[[El Hada Rocha]]**, a winged fairy luchador played by DeadAussieGamer. The night began with an argument about hair. Along the way it took in a field of hurled grandmothers, a flaming coconut crab, a beheaded donkey piñata and a room full of live electricity. It ended in a **double knockout**, with both champions falling on the same exchange.

## The Story

### The Grandmaster's New Look

The night opened in the halls of [[Grandmaster Elusia Skypiercer|the Grandmaster]], where [[The Arbiter|the Arbiter]] strode down a corridor to a door so large that everyone who walked past it said, "wow, that's a big door." Inside her private study, a tall, slender woman with blonde hair was browsing the shelves. The Arbiter didn't recognize her. "What the hell do you think you're doing in the Grandmaster's private study?" he demanded.

It was, of course, the Grandmaster, sporting a new hair color that was "literally the opposite color." The Arbiter floundered ("It's a nice dress, though"), insisted he always noticed when champions changed their hair because "it's kind of my job," and confessed he had judged tonight's fighters by their hairstyles. Unimpressed by his "driveling," the Grandmaster put her book back and ordered, "Bring the champions to me now."

### Two Beautiful Champions

The Arbiter clapped open a portal. First through it was El Hada Rocha, from [[The Feywild|the Feywilds]], "the champion of the peacock spirit." He wore skin-tight spandex and a luchador mask crowned with sweeping peacock feathers. When he flexed, enormous rainbow dragonfly wings tore out of the back of his suit. Then Cosmonius trotted in like a show horse at dressage. He was a chestnut centaur with the torso of a Greek Adonis, shampoo-commercial hair and a silky handlebar moustache, and he introduced himself as "the Galloping Grappler."

The Grandmaster circled both of them, entranced, and admitted the Arbiter had been right about the hair. Then Cosmonius told her he hadn't seen anyone so dashing since the Seelie Court. "Okay, I found my winner," she declared on the spot. The trash talk began immediately. El Hada Rocha scolded the centaur for showing up naked in front of the Grandmaster. Cosmonius answered with finger guns aimed at an audience that wasn't there. He assumed someone, somewhere, was always scrying on him, so he repeated his own name for anyone just tuning in. When El Hada Rocha suggested he "start calling yourself Glue," Cosmonius answered "I think not!" and wreathed himself in a cloak of colorful leaves, a fey blessing, before they had even reached an arena.

### Down to the Generator Room

The Grandmaster wanted to stay close to home, so the Arbiter made the floor stones rearrange themselves into a doorway. Cosmonius praised the house. "Thank you. I built it myself," said the Grandmaster. "Well, the [[Goblins|goblins]] offered a lot of help." Far below, the air turned icy and gears ground all around. "This, dear champions, is our generator room. Everything here powers the Dread Fortress." The fighting floor was a metal platform ringed by three-foot railings, above a drop onto a bed of spinning gears. El Hada Rocha apologized that there were no drains for the blood, and suggested the goblins clean up afterwards.

The Arbiter then picked his favorite. "You can't not trust a man who's wearing a mask," he reasoned, and he tossed El Hada Rocha a coin purse with "something special" inside. El Hada Rocha tied it to his mask and dared Cosmonius to come and take it. The centaur charged. The Grandmaster dropped between them and promised him her blessing if he drew first blood. She then summoned [[The Acolytes]] into a floating grandstand above the machinery. It was dollar hot dog night, and the crowd was already chanting "CHAOS!" Cosmonius laughed along with her evil laugh and was told off: nobody laughs when the Grandmaster laughs. She flew up to her throne beside [[Doug the Pug|Doug]].

El Hada Rocha moved first. With a flick of his hair, a swarm of fireflies burst from his mask and soured Cosmonius's fortune, and a floor tile cracked under the centaur's hooves.

### Puppets and Meemaws

El Hada Rocha opened with a pelvic thrust to the heavens that launched a blast shaped like a giant peacock. It missed. He set his feathers alight and left a trail of turquoise fire as he flew. "You're turning yourself into the DeLorean," Cosmonius observed. The Arbiter took the joke and ran with it: once the luchador's luck added up to 88, he would be able to bend time. Cosmonius covered the distance in a single gallop, landed two punches to the face and danced away trailing flower petals. "Come and get me, little man!" He had drawn first blood, and the Grandmaster kept her word. El Hada Rocha wiped purple blood from his lip, told the centaur he hit well "for someone who dresses like a poor person," and breathed peacock-colored fire over him. Cosmonius had to quickly stamp out his smoldering tail.

The crowd had earned two chaos events. The first was a gift from an Acolyte named Kalindra. Threads ran from the Arbiter's fingers to both champions' limbs and turned them into his puppets. He made Cosmonius punch himself in the face, which he dodged, since that is hard to do to a monk. He made El Hada Rocha open the coin purse and drink the healing vial hidden inside. For the second event the Grandmaster cried, "Give us your Meemaws!" and the Acolytes hurled their grandmothers into the arena. Ten grannies landed, all of them looking oddly like goblins. Anyone who passed one either got smacked with a purse or got a kiss on the cheek that healed them. "We here at D20 Deathmatch do not endorse the actual throwing of grandmothers," the Arbiter clarified.

El Hada Rocha proved himself, above all, a good grandson. He doused his flames so he wouldn't singe anyone and offered his arm to the nearest abuela. That abuela was B. Harthur, who whacked him with her purse: "I can do this on my own, Sonny!" Cosmonius, meanwhile, trotted past another granny and got a kiss on the cheek and a "My, you are very hot, aren't you?" Then he grappled El Hada Rocha with a perfect natural 20, swept his legs out from under him and stamped down on him twice with his free hoof. "Now discover why they call me the Harrowing Hooves." When Cosmonius rolled another 13, the Acolytes threw in their Peepaws as well. Then came Grandma Siren, a merfolk "cougar grandma" who sang "Brick House" and drew both fighters toward her. That made Cosmonius's kissing grandma jealous. "Don't you dare listen to that hussy!" she cried, and slapped the blessing right off his face.

### The Crab

The Arbiter could not stand to watch his favorite get pummelled any longer. He plucked feathers from El Hada Rocha's mask with a spectral hand and wove them into a nest-shaped shield. El Hada Rocha thanked him, and then the shield burst into flame. The fire spread over his whole body and took the shape of **an immense peacock-blue coconut crab**. The Grandmaster used the moment to remind everyone that STDs, like crabs, are very rampant among the elderly, so check in on your family. Cosmonius had his own complaint: "You know how hard it is to get crabs out of this much fur?"

The nearby grandparents reacted in different ways. One grandma screamed, wet her pants and attacked the crab. One grandpa cheered it on and reminisced about "the first time I had crabs." El Hada Rocha protested that this was like Becca's quinceañera all over again and that you can't tell people about this when you've just met them; the grandpa explained that "when two people love each other, sometimes crustaceans are involved," until the Grandmaster begged him to stop talking. Cosmonius kicked the crab's eyestalks, and the crab shrugged off his stunning strike with a natural 20: "The only thing that stuns me is your futility." He kept going anyway, and a flying double hoof-stomp finally cracked its shell. When he landed among the elderly, a veteran grandpa punched him hard; Grandpa had clearly "seen some shit." Before long the whole field had descended into a grandma-grandpa civil war over whose cookies were better ("Your macaroni tastes like shit!") and old grudges over card and word games. "Take these hussies off of my floor right now," the Grandmaster ordered. The Arbiter opened a portal and shuffled every grandparent into a retirement home in Florida.

### Never Look a Gift Horse in the Mouth

The crab collapsed back into a battered luchador, still carrying his feather shield. He spun onto his back and launched a "Phoenix dropkick," firing blasts from the soles of both feet like "a Gatling gun of feet." Every single one missed. "Arbiter, couldn't you have found someone a little more on my level?" Cosmonius sneered. "He was wearing a mask… I trusted him," the Arbiter pleaded. Out of pity, the Grandmaster let him try one blast again, and Cosmonius dropped his guard: "Go on, hit me." El Hada Rocha stomped out his burning feet like a cigarette, breakdanced into a flaming kick and knocked the centaur's jaw out of line.

Cosmonius answered with his signature "Chopsbuster" punch. It missed, and so did his follow-up kicks, which only hit the Arbiter's shield. That gave him the line that named the night:

> "It seems like the Arbiter's gift to you is the only thing keeping you here… you know what they say? Never look a gift horse in the mouth." (Cosmonius)

He then punched himself in the jaw to heal it back into place. El Hada Rocha grabbed his hair, swung up onto his back circus-style and rode him around the arena while waving to the crowd. Cosmonius tried to shove him off, and he ended up side-saddle.

### Shocks and a Piñata

Between blows the two of them drifted into an impromptu therapy session about their grandmothers. El Hada Rocha admitted that "me and Poppy had a very bad relationship," and remembered making tripitas with his grandma. Then he set off a thunderclap on the centaur's back that left Cosmonius hobbling. Bored with the scenery, the Grandmaster asked for something more electrifying. The Arbiter pulled lightning down into the machinery, and the whole generator room erupted like fireworks. Cosmonius was in mid-leap and took only part of the shock. He then back-kicked the "fairyman" over the edge, only to discover that the wings actually worked. (At the table, his player admitted he had thought they were decorative.)

The crowd's chaos counter had "never expected to go above five," and the sixth event was a giant donkey piñata on a string. Cosmonius blinked onto it, grabbed it by the ears and, with another natural 20, tore its whole head off. He wore the head as a helmet. "Piñata King! I'm adding that to the list of names." He found four full-size candy bars inside, tucked two into his hand wraps and ate one. Then he dove hooves-first onto his rival. El Hada Rocha, plucked and bruised and spattered with pixie blood, roared, "You, you dare step on El Hada Rocha?" Cosmonius replied, "Oh, I dare." The luchador grabbed an electrified pylon and flung its current at the centaur in the shape of a peacock's wingspan. Then his luck finally passed 88. "Past 88 miles per hour," the Arbiter announced. "You are the master of time and space."

### Like Fae

Cosmonius caught his rival in a headlock under his armpit and dropped his whole horse body sideways into a choke. El Hada Rocha's wings turned into blue peacock-flame feathers and wrapped the centaur in vengeful fire. With his insect wings now broken, he rose fifty feet on blue flame and dove like an arrow, firing from his feet, and landed a crushing explosion. Cosmonius tried a capoeira sweep at the last second but mistimed it and took the whole blast.

Both champions were left hanging on by a thread. Cosmonius offered a fist bump. "It will be sad to see you go." El Hada Rocha reached up with trembling, broken fingers and bumped back. "Shall we go out like men then? Put up your dukes." Cosmonius answered, "No. Not like men. Like Fae. Like Fae!" A straight jab lifted El Hada Rocha off the ground. With his last breath of magic he answered with one final rebuke of fire, and it dropped the centaur. Cosmonius stood for a second, then a hoof gave way and he toppled forward. El Hada Rocha floated back into the metal wall with his flaming wings guttering out. He fell to his knees, pulled off his mask and hit the floor.

"It was an awful waste of hair," the Arbiter mourned, before noting that there had at least been a death, which is "technically" the only rule. The Grandmaster flew to the center of the arena and spoke to the crowd.

> "Dear acolytes, I fear I have no champion to give you… Remember the names of El Hada Rocha and Cosmonius. You have witnessed true greatness. And next time I see you all in the stands, may your hair be just as glorious." (The Grandmaster)

## Dried Blood

Given a final word to the Acolytes, both champions stayed on brand. El Hada Rocha reminded his fans that beauty lies not on the outside but in the spirit and heart, "because that is where you find the true peacock god." Cosmonius asked only to be remembered "as I was, beautiful and flawless."
