For the Grandmaster's birthday, there was no ordinary duel. **[[Dan Kowalski]]**, a party-loving satyr, and **[[Gordy Gotta Get Em]]**, a small, twitchy stabber, were sent racing side by side through **[[The Jungle Gauntlet]]**, her oldest training ground, under a simple rule: first across lives, last dies. Along the way came murderous squirrels, a dragon named Gerard, a foreskin-wrapped wand, a lovesick Babau and a carrot called Crazy Eyes Tony. It made for a strange, sweet and brutal birthday party.

## The Story

### A Birthday in the Fortress

The night opened on a celebration in **[[The Dread Fortress]]**: it was the birthday of **[[Grandmaster Elusia Skypiercer|the Grandmaster]]**, who admitted to being "a little bit older. Not a lot." **[[The Arbiter]]**, calling himself "a pretty smooth talker," handed her a small box. She opened it very carefully, half-expecting a jack-in-the-box to lunge at her. Inside sat a swirling black-and-purple crystal like a captured galaxy. He called it "a particularly favorite item of mine," a thing that displaces time and space and can change the health and defenses of two opponents at any Deathmatch she likes. He offered to take it back if she didn't care for it. "No, no, no. I want it," she said. She declared it "absolutely diabolical," and, sipping from her pineapple cup, added: "This is evil, and I appreciate it."

Her birthday wish was to go "back a little bit to the beginning," to "my oldest of training grounds… where I first began my own growth as a warrior." She also wanted worthy champions: "I am, after all, 284. I deserve something." Luckily, the Arbiter had "met some guys at the bowling alley the other week… They would love to die for you, I think."

Gordy entered first, stammering: "My name's Gordy, um, I gotta get em. I just gotta stab. Mostly stab." Asked what the very first thing he ever "got" was, he answered: "A circumcision actually." The Grandmaster took this in stride and offered free circumcisions to any Acolyte afterwards. Then came Dan: "Dan Kowalski, Danimal on the weekends, Daniel to my mother," a satyr with "sick horns," clip-clopping happily. Asked what her party needed, he answered "Punch, pie, and Danimal!" He then pulled a wargong out of a magic pocket and gave a full solo performance, hitting it from every side. "It's been a while since we've had a gong solo in the Dread Fortress," the Arbiter noted. Satisfied that both were "worthy and at least interesting," the Grandmaster asked to be taken to the jungle. The Arbiter clapped, and the sound waves tore open a portal.

### Welcome to the Jungle

The jungle was where the young Grandmaster had once trained alone, running its lanes "when I had no one to go up against." Each champion took his own lane, Dan to the north. They could see and talk to each other the whole way, and they had to win through with battle and cleverness. "If you're not first, you're last," she announced. "In this case, dead." She then took her seat in the stands beside **[[Doug the Pug]]**, who was barking his head off over his own dog-friendly cupcake, and thanked **[[The Acolytes]]** for coming.

Dan bounded over the opening log like "an ibex" (the Grandmaster: "Much cuter"), sparing a grateful grub. He was immediately pelted by furious squirrels hurling "vicious and sharp acorns." He then pinned Gordy in place with grasping earthen hands, only for his own hooves to slip on the cliff. He slid all the way to the bottom "just eating dirt," which he claimed "tastes like kombucha."

Gordy fought back by planting his voice in Dan's head, psychically chanting "gotta get em, gotta get em" to rattle him. Dan stayed unbothered and invited Gordy bowling afterwards: "Next time, I definitely want to use the pink ball, man. Six pounds." Gordy wriggled free with "the old suck it satyr special." Because he had lingered so long, more squirrels showed up to pelt him too. "Nothing here is cute or friendly," the Grandmaster warned. "Trust nothing." The squirrels shouted insults back, which she refused to translate because they were "incredibly rude."

Then a gang of middle-schoolers in the stands started heckling both champions about everything: "He's got dumb hair. He's got feminine hips." Both men took it hard. "Hey, I know I don't have birthday gifts, okay?" Dan cried. Gordy summed it up: "Children are the worst. I may be the same size as them, but they just radiate so much more evil."

### Snakes, Lily Pads and Mom Jokes

The friendship frayed on the cliff. Dan flung fire and missed, and Gordy started calling him "Daniel." "Don't you call me Daniel, okay?" Gordy raced to the clifftop and tossed a psychic blade into him, then ran straight into four flying snakes ("I've made an error"). One flew past his face, smacked into the rock and died. "It was a dumb one," the Grandmaster observed.

Dan's attempt to put Gordy to sleep fizzled, and Gordy faked a snooze just to taunt him. Then Dan met his own line of snakes. "Why did it have to be snakes?" Rolling a perfect 20, he sweet-talked them: "Hey, snakes. Why don't you go to Gordy? Because he really wants to get you." The whole swarm nodded along and slithered over to Gordy's lane. "They don't speak common," the Arbiter explained, "but those hips don't lie."

At the raging river of lily pads, Dan slipped, bit his own tongue, and got chomped by an alligator that would not be persuaded to go bother "a guy over there named Gordy. He's a fucking asshole." Gordy teleported past the snakes, spotted the dodgy lily pad, and stayed dry: "I'm drier than your mom, Daniel." On the far bank, the five squirrels had followed him and unloaded their entire food supply on him. "Daniel, we need to team up and destroy these squirrels." Dan declined, stepped through a magical door to the treeline, and flipped Gordy off with a mom joke. Moments later he relented: "Like my friend Aristotle once said, let it be."

### The Farting Forest and the Box of Chaos

Next was a forest of unnaturally bright blue and yellow plants that puffed toxic pollen with every gust ("farting plants, essentially"). The Acolytes, thrilled, granted each champion a gift of their favor and demanded **[[The Box of Chaos|the Box of Chaos]]**, which the Grandmaster set on the forest's far side.

Gordy dashed straight through, breathing deep. He came out poisoned and glowing blue: "Suck it, Daniel. I'm magic now." At the Box, flaming with cool fire, he sacrificed "the ceremonial goblin dagger that's used for all circumcisions. Its handle wrapped in the foreskin of my forefathers." The Box shook its approval and gave him a Wand of Secrets with a matching hilt. A manticore lunged, missed, bit a tree stump, and cracked a tooth.

Dan, who "knows his horticulture," took the patient route. He summoned a tidal wave that soaked the pollen into the earth and strolled through untouched. For his sacrifice he hacked off a piece of his own horn. The Box made a near-vocal "mmm," laughed chaotically, and produced a Sphere of Annihilation. "Is this like one of those balls from that David Bowie movie?" Dan asked. "Cool bowling ball, Dan," said Gordy.

Then a lamb (or was it a puppet?) began singing "This is the song that never ends" on endless loop. Gordy and his manticore fell into a trance, and a gathering of ducklings waddled onto the field.

### Gerard, the Hag and the Vegetables

To impress the birthday girl, Dan ribbon-danced and summoned a fiery red dragon with antlers like his own, one of them now short. "Grandmaster, for your birthday, I present to you Gerard the Dragon." ("That's not a dragon. That's a Wyrmling," the Arbiter objected. The Grandmaster: "No, he's a dragon. Hi, Gerard the Dragon.") Gerard flubbed both attacks out of stage fright, then torched the manticore.

Gordy woke to a seasoned manticore's perfect bite and begged to go home. The Grandmaster blessed him instead: "Don't you dare back down from my gauntlet. Put some metal into your bones. Buck up, buddy." He killed Dan's manticore, which turned out to be geriatric and relieved: "Damn, I'm glad this was my time. I've had enough."

Gordy then found an old woman tied to a tree. He called her "a very beautiful young maiden," checked her with his wand (one of its foreskin wrappings came off, slithered to the tip, flew off and burst into salt) and cut her free. She banished his manticore, healed him, and hugged him, and he hugged her back ("Oh my gosh, this is the greatest day ever"). "Do you have any idea how many travelers have come past me every time and never given me anything?" she said, before vanishing. Meanwhile a troll in scuba gear rose from the river and set a plate of wilted, sludge-covered veggies before each champion: "Eat your veggies." Dan had Gerard rotisserie his and ate them. Gordy ignored his, and his spurned vegetables grew enormous and inched after him into the cave. A broccoli and Crazy Eyes Tony the carrot ("because of the way that I'm orange") pleaded with him for just one bite. "This is the worst PBS nightmare I could think of."

Bidding Gerard farewell at the narrow cave mouth ("Please assist that big pile of poo"), Dan stuck a candle in his broken horn as a headlamp. He asked a passing ghost to give his condolences to his friend James, and a second ghost replied "Who is James?" He took a weapon that exploded "in the smallest sort of way." Then he found a chest holding an emerald that melted into his skin and healed him. Gordy found two of his own and gave one to the Grandmaster: "I offered you my foreskin. You didn't like that. So how about the stone?" Doug hopped down and fetched it in his little mouth. Both champions stepped through the same portal.

### The Lovesick Babau

They landed together in a dungeon with no visible exit, where a Babau leapt from a balcony. When it turned its weakening gaze on Dan, he flirted back ("I think you're cute, but I'm taken"). The grey beast blushed bright pink and for a while would only attack Gordy. "Those feminine hips are entrancing. It's flustered by your sexual appeal," the Grandmaster said.

Dan then set the Sphere of Annihilation down between Gordy and the Babau and walked off. Gordy slashed him on the way past with a perfect strike ("Technically, you're a bitch"). Dan answered by blasting the Sphere. It erupted on Gordy and the Babau alike before collapsing into a black-hole singularity and vanishing. "Gordy, how did you explode next to that creature? I don't even know."

The wounded Babau turned on Dan in "a lover's fury" and dropped him to the brink of death. Dan spent the Acolytes' gift on a magical door beneath himself and escaped. Hidden behind a pillar, Gordy spent his own gift on a wall run up the column and came down with both daggers. "Wall run, Gordy's gotta get em!" The Babau slumped dead, and the crowd went wild.

### The Final Blow

But two still stood. Dan clawed back to his feet, barely alive, and crept up on the celebrating Gordy. This time his sleep spell worked, and Gordy dropped where he stood. "Seeing what you did to my beloved Babau," Dan declared, and stomped his cloven hooves. Lightning cracked the stone, and Gordy's particles dissipated into the energy. The blast threw Dan skyward, and the Grandmaster caught him in midair.

The Arbiter swept Gordy's remains into a sack and walked away whistling: "Got him." A single tear fell from the Grandmaster's eye as a smile spread across her face. The Acolytes chanted "Dan, Dan, Dan" until the stands shook. "Here is our first winner of the gauntlet," she proclaimed, "the first to make its path and live to tell the tale, since yours truly had survived it."

## Dried Blood

In his final words, Gordy bore no grudge: "Happy birthday, Grandmaster. You're a lovely, lovely overlord. And we gotta go bowling in the afterlife." The Grandmaster promised to put up some pins for him. The Arbiter offered a simpler epitaph, "One is born and one dies." Dan closed with one more borrowed wisdom, from "my good friend Leonardo da Vinci." At the table, the hosts admitted the cave had held surprises nobody reached, including giant spiders, and that nobody had ever been put to sleep in a match before.
