Two towering exes, a goliath from the Witchlight Carnival and a satyr graduate of [[Strixhaven]], were dropped into a snow globe of a town and told to settle things for good. What began as glitter bombs, goblin kidnappings and trash talk ended with ugly crying and a forehead kiss. For one night, the [[Grandmaster Elusia Skypiercer|Grandmaster]] let love take the win instead of blood.

## The Story

### Hallmark at the Dread Fortress
The season opened in the halls of [[The Dread Fortress]], where [[The Arbiter|the Arbiter]] found the Grandmaster slouched across her throne, legs over one arm, glaring at an old TV. She was watching a Hallmark Christmas movie, the one where someone moves back home, the trees go up and the snow falls. She had had enough. She threw her remote at the screen: "I'm just sick of this! They give us the same romance story again and again… I want true romance. I want love and heat and fire." Then she ran over and knocked the television flat. [[Goblins]] scurried to pick it up. "It's fine. It's an old one."

The Arbiter had noticed her binge of rom-coms and had brought her "something more true to life." She asked whether he had ever been in love. "I had a girlfriend once… You don't know her. She went to a different school." The Grandmaster's reply was immediate: "Did she know she was your girlfriend?" Undeterred, he presented his offering: "a lover's quarrel… Lethe versus Sorry."

### Two Towering Exes
**[[Lethe]]** arrived groggy, having been pulled out of sleep at the carnival, and suspected another carnival trick. Then they spotted who was waiting. **[[Sorry]]** was just as puzzled. The last thing she remembered was being in a dungeon. "Oh, right," said Lethe. "At wizard school." The old wound opened straight away: "You told me that I wasn't smart enough to go to wizard school… That's why we broke up." Sorry insisted there was a difference: "It's not that you weren't smart enough. It's just I didn't think that you could do it."

The Grandmaster saw "a little bit of bad blood and story to resolve" and invited them to settle it before [[The Acolytes|her Acolytes]]. Lethe introduced themself as a lifelong resident of the Witchlight Carnival who could move things with their mind and with their arms, "I am after all seven feet fucking tall." Sorry, a ripped 6'5" satyr in a deep V-neck and colorful scarves, confessed that she knew exactly one spell, comprehend languages, and cast it by eating the paper. That was how she had passed every test at Strixhaven, a school built on regurgitating information. Lethe asked whether she meant literally regurgitating. "Sometimes," Sorry admitted: that one essay on bugbears "kinda didn't sit very well." Lethe had a theory: "I think the only reason you passed is because your professors couldn't actually find the papers to grade."

The Arbiter picked the arena: the Grandmaster's old winter town, a personal vacation spot she shares "with none others." Snow drifted down, the kind that makes you want to snuggle up to someone. "Those days are long past," said Lethe. "We're inside of a proverbial snow globe," said the Arbiter. "You never know." The Grandmaster flew up to her throne beside [[Doug the Pug]], and the two champions landed in the very same alleyway. Fate, the Arbiter announced, would not let them stay apart for long.

### Glitter and Grudges
Sorry struck first in the showiest way possible. She vaulted clean over Lethe in a mirthful leap, pulling a face down at them as they swung uselessly at the air. Then she punched a bolted door, which shook off its snow and refused to budge. She flew into a rage, and because she is a wild magic barbarian, her fury summoned a pixie spirit that hovered over Lethe's head and made the same face. "Are you serious? Little trinkets again?"

The Acolytes demanded chaos and got the Mad Hatter, a disheveled old man in mismatched suspenders and a very large hat. He ran between the champions, shouted "Change places!" and vanished, scattering them across town. Lethe complained that this was just like the carnival. The Grandmaster explained that the carnival really had come by the week before, as a thank-you to the goblins.

Lethe gathered themself. Their shadow pooled at their feet and climbed their body, hardening into a carapace, and their eyes glowed cold white. "Fine. You want to fight, darling? Let's fight. Follow me." They swatted the pixie down the street like a baseball and stepped into a magic potion shop full of trinkets and a swirling basin of ethereal mist. It reminded them of the week the carnival visited Strixhaven, when they waited outside Sorry's potion class. So they planted their glaive and waited again.

With Lethe out of sight, Sorry's confidence briefly cracked. She recovered with a toothy grin at the shop door, her shield bearing the emblem of Lorehold, her old college: "I'm here. You called? Bring it." Another pixie exploded on Lethe in a glitter bomb of soft blue, pink and creamy white. "Scars will heal," the Grandmaster observed. "But glitter?" Lethe knew: "Glitter never comes out."

Lethe had learned some tricks of their own. They flicked the glitter back at Sorry and laid a hex on her, turning it into sludgy dark ice that clung to her armor. Sorry had never seen them cast a spell. "I wish that you showed more of this ferocity when we were together," she said. "You were so soft, Sorry," Lethe replied. "I was afraid I'd break you in half." There was a pang in her eyes, and also a blush.

### Goblins in the Floor
The next chaos event popped up checkered tiles with little eyes peeking out: "It's goblin snatching time!" Goblins swarmed both champions, hoping to take them hostage and make them pay for their freedom. Both shook them off, but the goblins lurked in the wings, hoping to earn a little extra coin.

Blades locked. "I can bang on this all day long," Sorry boasted. "I've got the stamina. Can you keep up?" Lethe: "It was me that you could never keep up with. Sorry." Sorry answered by winking and blowing them a kiss, and Lethe remembered that she had only ever toyed with them. Their face curled into a snarl. When the next pixie blast shoved them into her, they hit back with telekinesis and hurled Sorry thirty feet. It was too far, because Lethe was still flustered from the kiss. A goblin waved as she sailed past. Sorry was shocked, and maybe a little impressed.

Doug's tongue rolled out when Sorry asked whether she could pet him after she slaughtered her ex. The Grandmaster ruled that the victor would get to pet him and take him on a walk. Meanwhile a short goblin swung a scimitar at Sorry and missed, fuming that "[[Kenny]]'s got to pay his rent."

### The Potion Shop Explodes
After one too many pixie blasts, the shop's bottles burst. Sorry dodged the whole spray and found a healing potion on the floor. Lethe had thrown their body over her on pure reflex. "What are you doing?" "Nothing! We're fighting to the death, that's what we're doing." The Grandmaster was not fooled: "That is a sign of love."

Poison and glitter mixed into a gas of decay that ate at Lethe's carapace, and their flustered swings glanced off Sorry's shield. Then, locked in close, Sorry sniffed them. "Are you using a different shampoo?… Never mind!" Lethe was stunned: "You remember what my shampoo smelled like?"

Sensing the conflict, the Grandmaster spoke into Lethe's head: "Trust your heart. Whatever action you choose… it must always be done in confidence." Lethe did something they had never done before. Their tattoos moved and glowed into a map of the skeleton beneath their skin, and glowing white tears streamed down their face as they took on an aspect of their patron's dread. "I was never good enough for you. And this, I'm good enough for this." Sorry: "You don't look okay."

A long-armed goblin chose this moment to pin Lethe and demand payment. "Cost me what? I've already given up everything that matters." "That's super sad. What's in your pockets?" He saw that Lethe's little song horn was sentimental and took the hand axes and a flask of cranberry juice instead. "Next time, put better things in your pockets." Sorry declared victory on the spot, since every real fighter knows the power comes from hydration.

### Chairs from Behind the Throne
After the break, [[The Box of Chaos]] dropped from the sky in pink and yellow flames, offering a magic item for the right sacrifice. Then came another switch-up. The Grandmaster rated the Arbiter's roll the same as she'd rate him: a 4. "You sound like my mom," he said. "She and I talk often," she replied, and HR, a small terrified goblin in the corner, totally approved it.

From behind her throne, her "Mary Poppins bag of danger," she produced two metal chairs. The Arbiter soared down and whipped his onto Lethe, who half-caught it. "You are all ridiculous." "This is my career… don't ruin this for me." The Grandmaster dove like a falcon at Sorry and missed, but her chair threw up a wave of snow that caught her anyway. The Arbiter resolved to call Dave about another chair-fu class.

### The Breakdown
Sorry had stopped having fun. She finally landed a real blow, and looked almost regretful as she pulled her bloody blade back. Lethe answered with a flurry of glaive strikes, but the last ones carried regret too. As blood was drawn, Lethe staggered: "What are we doing?"

Sorry's wild magic surged into rainbow protective lights, and she began ugly crying. In those lights Lethe saw the colors of the carnival, and the question they had never asked: what if you were at the carnival too? Sorry kept swinging with her eyes shut, screaming "I'M GONNA KILL YOU!" Lethe stepped back: "Do you want to, or are we just trying to kill each other because those two weird gods pulled us into a pocket dimension and told us to?"

It all came out. Sorry had graduated, thank you, and was now "a glorified babysitter" whose little ones were probably lost in a dungeon without her. Lethe admitted they had wanted to ask her to come with the carnival, but took her words to mean they didn't fit together. "So I left." Sorry lowered her sword: "Of course you don't fit in at Strixhaven. You're better than all of them."

Lethe's glowing bones flickered out, and a blush rose where they had been. They knew Sorry couldn't tell lies, "unless you took care of that curse." "Wait, I'm cursed?" Lethe laughed. They admitted that they had spent so long trying to prove they could be something that they never thought about who they wanted to be. "I decided to just be angry." Sorry preferred "unbridled passion." Lethe had a suggestion: "Maybe we could reach into that weird box and get the fuck out of here? Maybe try to start over?" They could deal with their curses together. Blushing furiously, Sorry stammered that it was safe to do these kinds of things together, and Lethe agreed: safety in numbers.

### Love Will Win
The Grandmaster, who had been eavesdropping, leaned into the doorway. "This battle was a choice, a chance for you to find your heart and where it lies." At her glance the Box of Chaos vanished. It could not give them closure or safety. "That you will only find together." She admitted that only Doug had ever brought warmth to her heart, then knelt in the snow before them both.

> "Today, I can't believe I'm choosing to say this, but today, love will win." — the Grandmaster

She offered them her retreat, her home away from home, as a place to bond and grow, and promised that the Arbiter, "a master of time," would return them to whatever time they chose when they were ready. Sorry wiped the snot off her face and wondered aloud whether to mention that she's just an aggressive crybaby by default. Lethe kissed her forehead, and her little deer tail wagged. "Maybe we complement each other pretty well." The Grandmaster lifted them before the Acolytes as two champions, celebrating tears as fiercely as blood. Doug barked in circles at the prospect of double the walkies.

## Dried Blood
After the show, Sorry offered some advice. Therapy is great, but in the meantime it's fine to punch a few doors, or even get into a deathmatch with your ex. You might get to pet a magic pug at the end. She'd read something like it on a piece of paper, "and, you know, I ate that." Lethe's advice was gentler. Holding on to someone else's idea of who you are only buries you deeper, and you have to remember who you want to be. "And sometimes that means saying sorry."
