A toffee-dispensing grandma and a giant, spoiled barbarian with flames drawn on his space helmet were dropped onto a tiny orange asteroid in [[The Ether Drifts|the Ether Drifts]] to fight to the death. What followed was half Deathmatch and half family therapy. There were illusory dads, a killer cat, a vengeful raccoon and sixty crushed worms. Then came a twist so strange that [[Grandmaster Elusia Skypiercer|the Grandmaster]] compared it to a Greek tragedy, and she ended the night crowning **[[Agatha Middlebrooke]]** and **[[Lug Hardblock]]** together.

## The Story

### Before the Fight
The night began badly. After a crash in the fortress's systems, [[The Arbiter|the Arbiter]] announced that "Stuart is about to get fucking fired." The Grandmaster was more forgiving: [[Stuart]] only needed to research new solutions, and he would get a bonus if the show made it to the end without him pulling any more plugs.

Deeper in [[The Dread Fortress|the Dread Fortress]], metal clanged and stone crumbled. [[Thea]], the imp, was wedged in a narrow hallway as a giant pink Tyrannosaurus Rex. She had been stuck that way since a polymorph the week before, and she was in pain. "I don't wanna be like this anymore!" she wailed. "It wasn't supposed to last this long! Why am I still a dinosaur?!" Her thrashing trampled the Arbiter's feet ("Oh, no, those are Italian leather!"). He climbed onto her back and smacked her on the head, and she melted like silly putty back into her imp form. "Easy fix. Just a knockoff wand."

The Grandmaster's heels clicked down the hall. "Do you see my fortress? It is in shambles." She blamed the Arbiter, who had put the wand in [[The Box of Chaos|the Box of Chaos]] for a champion to find. He refused the blame: "Listen, I put a guy's soul in that box. I'm not responsible for what comes out of the box." She wanted the box controlled, perhaps with a pocket dimension to pull from. He insisted "Nobody's in charge of the box. It's chaos in a box," and then muttered to Thea that the Grandmaster had no idea how chaos works. Ticket sales would have to rebuild the fortress, and in the Arbiter's view the whole mess was Stuart's fault.

### Grandma and the Biggest Man
The Arbiter flared his white glowing wings, clapped, and ripped a portal open. Through it came a sweet old lady ("Oh goodness, deary me. What strapping young people we have here today") and a hulking barbarian who led the room in a "Hoo-yah." When the Grandmaster greeted the "champions," Lug corrected her: "Correction, champion. Singular." The Arbiter liked him at once.

Agatha's kindness was relentless, and it drove Lug up the wall. She was proud of him for building his confidence. She called him "such a big man." When she mentioned her bad knees, he announced, "I'm jotting that down. I'm going to use that against you." Then she handed the Arbiter a toffee "for your lovely portal," and he was so touched he turned to the Grandmaster: "Why can't you be nice like this? All you do is yell at me."

They stepped into the blackness of space. The arena was one small asteroid among many, and [[The Acolytes|the Acolytes]] floated above in zero-gravity grandstands, wearing helmets with tubes to get their beers inside. The Grandmaster put on her own helmet and gave the usual instruction: "Whatever you do, make sure you're the only one who leaves this planet alive."

### Dads, Dads Everywhere
Lug charged first. As he ran he grew to two or three times his size, eclipsing the little grandma like the sun. The Grandmaster asked whether this was what steroids looked like and confirmed the Deathmatch has no anti-doping rules: "No, we absolutely do not." Lug wound up "like I'm hitting a golf ball" and launched Agatha off the asteroid into open space. With no gravity to stop her, she would have drifted forever. Instead she vanished in a little poof of lavender and reappeared on solid ground. Lug protested that magic shouldn't be allowed. "You did steroids," Agatha replied.

Lug kept bringing up his father. His dad was never disappointed in him ("I'm his special boy"), and his dad had taught him that golf swing. Agatha decided she could help. The air around her turned to fog, and a dozen or more perfect copies of Lug's dad appeared. Each was broad-shouldered, square-jawed and going a little silver, "classically handsome in a way that Lug didn't quite get." Agatha even wore his face over her own. All of them spoke at once: "I'm proud of you, son."

Lug believed every word. "I've been waiting for so long to hear you say that. I'm going to win this for you, Papa." As tears ran down his face, a cat came flying at him from his father's own arm. It was **Lord Cookie Mill**, Agatha's familiar, a sphinx that was "for all intents and purposes a cat," trained through the Westhaven Book Club. It latched onto Lug's face and tore open a nostril. "Oh, Lord Cookie Mill, bad kitty," Agatha fretted. Lug reassured the illusions: "It's okay. I'm really good at killing cats, just like you taught me."

He tried to hurl the cat into space, but it clung on and climbed all over him. "You are now currently a cat tree," the Grandmaster observed. He finally swatted it away. When he went to hug one of the dads, the illusion melted in his arms, like the opening of the Ark in Raiders of the Lost Ark. "I'll get you this, witch," he snarled, and then insisted the tears were from his injuries. Agatha gently suggested it was fine for men to cry. "Not me, not any male in my line. Therapy is for old people and poor people and I don't need it."

Agatha pointed at him and gave one firm command: "Share." Lug, an only child, did not share. His mind gave way anyway: "I'm thinking about how my father never really supported me… I don't think he's ever shown a moment of kindness or warmth towards me. What the hell is happening?" Meanwhile the cat's eyes had gone black like a shark's. The Arbiter admitted he had never spectated a therapy session before, and he wasn't hating it. The Acolytes fell in love with Agatha, cheering her name, flinging slushies that floated off into space, and tearing off their clothes.

### Jimothy
The Acolytes demanded chaos, and a tiny paw crept out of a crater. It belonged to **Jimothy**, "a slightly adorable deformed raccoon" with a nub of a tail and a few mangled teeth. Asked what a raccoon was doing on an asteroid, the Grandmaster said only, "Jimothy goes where Jimothy wants." He approached Lug timidly, hoping for a kind hand. Agatha shooed him with her broom ("If the HOA saw raccoons by the curb, they would be in trouble"), and Lug joined in. Jimothy sprayed both champions with acidic urine and fled. "You could have been blessed by Jimothy," the Grandmaster scolded. "You chose to attack it."

### The Wormhole
In the asteroid's cracks, Lug spotted a whole civilization of tiny multicolored worms living underground, the same people as the old worm town whose mayor, [[Timothy]], had died long ago. Lug saw only "more real estate to build high rise condominiums." His weapon burst into flame ("I told you I'm gonna burn you, old lady"), but the cat appeared between them, stared at him with its black eyes, and turned the blow back into his own mind. The Acolytes hated Lug so much that the rebounding pain tripled. "This is the strongest cat I've ever seen," Lug said. When Agatha praised his battle scars ("Who doesn't like a man that's had some experience?"), he begged her to stop saying nice things about him.

Then he spiked her. He sent her flying, chased her down, and hammered her straight into the crevasse. "I'm a whole goddamn volleyball team." She smashed through the worm town and crushed sixty worms "as the screams of their children and their loved ones cry out into the heavens." Agatha's response was the darkest line of the night: "Oh, well, at least your souls will feed the garden." Even Lug was rattled. "It's a wormhole!" cried the Grandmaster.

Covered in squished worms, Agatha floated gently out of the hole "like a little magical grandma." She had one question for Lug: "How do you think I knew what your father looked like?" Under the cat's purr, like a radio slightly out of tune, Lug heard two words: "pathetic boy." "Dad? Dad? I thought he loved me." Agatha handed out toffee and butterscotch, the Arbiter and the Grandmaster each took one, and the Grandmaster called a toffee break.

### Little Death
After the break, Lug explained that he had come first in everything his whole life "because my dad paid people off," from his first egg and spoon race to free college. He set out to crack Agatha's space helmet and suffocate her ("Is that cool?"). He swung his new axe, "the Lamborghini of great axes," while the Acolytes pelted him with slushies. Two blows split her faceplate to the top, and her air began to leak away.

Agatha looked at him calmly. "There comes a time in every grandma's life where she has to do the right thing… Good shot. He would have been proud." She drifted backwards, cast a spell called Little Death, and died, her body floating lifelessly above the asteroid. "Silencing women works," Lug declared. The Grandmaster raised his wrist with a forced smile and named him the winner. In his speech he promised to demolish Agatha's old people's home and turn it into a soup kitchen for the poor, "and then break that down and then build a high rise condominiums."

### Grandma's Ghost
The lights flickered. "That lady was not just some old grandma." A ghastly Agatha shone through from the Ethereal Plane. "Did he ever tell you about your grandmother? It is I, your grandma. And you killed me." Lug's father had kept her away from him, so she had cursed the man into her cat. "You've been fighting your dad this whole time," the Grandmaster realized.

The ghost slipped into Lug through his very strong chin and gave her grandson "the one thing that he was never able to give himself. A hug." The Acolytes watched Lug wrap his own arms around himself ("No, no, no, I hate this… this is what therapy is for") while the cat clawed at his face. Agatha whispered, "You can be a better man than your father was," and let him go. Bloodied and shrunk back to normal size, Lug heard her parting words, "Show your father that he is nothing to be afraid of," and she returned to her body, alive again.

### The Cat-Firework
Agatha did not fight. She wanted to see whether her grandson would stand up to his father "in the form of a little cat." "Whether you're actually my dad or just a regular cat, it doesn't matter. I'll kill you anyway," Lug said. While Agatha cheered "Let him grow! Give him grace! He's just a boy!", he tore the cat off himself and smacked it so hard that it burned up in the asteroid's thin air and burst like a firework. "And if that really was my dad, good riddance."

Agatha floated down and offered a choice: fight his grandma, or live free of the yoke his father had put on him. "The first time in my life, I don't need to win," Lug said. He would still build the condos, though. "Thank you, Grandma." She was glad, since now she wouldn't have to summon a spirit of death. They agreed to summon one later to celebrate the condos.

The Grandmaster, absolutely flustered, had no script for this. "There are many bonds that connect us. And that of family truly seems unbreakable. Unless you're a cat, then it turns out you're very breakable and you turn into a firework." Both were crowned champions. Lug left the Acolytes some beauty advice (tape your mouth at night), and Agatha shared a shoplifting tip, which Lug condemned on behalf of big corporations. The crowd booed, cheered and threw popcorn, some of it in celebration and some of it at the champions.

## Dried Blood
Agatha left the Acolytes with one last piece of grandmotherly wisdom: "Don't forget to be yourself, but also probably get a real job and, you know, don't be a fucking loser." The Grandmaster's verdict on the pair was "This family is the worst." The Arbiter noted that they're both alive, so the Void may see more of them.

## Who and What Appears
### Characters
**[[Characters/Agatha Middlebrooke|Agatha Middlebrooke]]** (Cinderblocksally). An old grandma and "classic spooky warlock" (per Dried Blood), with bad knees.
- **Background:** a member of the **Westhaven Book Club** (its council) and the local HOA. In her youth she was "a young rebel out in the mountains with the hemp and the boys."
- **Personality:** hands out toffees and butterscotch, and is relentlessly complimentary. She is nice to everyone except raccoons and one chatter.
- **Revealed to be Lug's grandmother.** Lug's father kept her away from Lug, so **she cursed him into her cat.**
- **Spells and powers:** Misty Step (lavender poof), Silent Image, Command, Levitate, and **Little Death** (she dies, returns as a ghost able to possess people, then revives at half her maximum HP). She claims she can summon a spirit of death.
- **Plan:** to summon a spirit of death and collect souls to celebrate Lug's condos.
- **Tips:** shoplifting from the bottom of the cart; "get a real job."

**[[Characters/Lug Hardblock|Lug Hardblock]]** (Zac). A rich, spoiled barbarian heel who grows enormous when he rages.
- **Gear and powers:** greataxe, warhammer, Elemental Cleaver (fire), Mighty Impel and pushing attacks, space helmet with flames drawn on.
- **Appearance:** one eye.
- **His father:** calls Lug "his special boy," taught him golf, bribed his way to Lug's firsts (an egg and spoon race, free college), and taught him to kill cats.
- **Personality:** an only child. Hates therapy, the poor and "handouts." Real-estate developer with plans for high-rise condos on the worm town and on Agatha's old home.
- **In this episode:** he killed the cat that held his father, chose not to fight his grandmother, and thanked her. Crowned a co-champion.

**Lug's father, "Papa"**: broad-shouldered, tall, both eyes, square jaw, going silver, classically handsome. **Cursed by Agatha into Lord Cookie Mill.** Whispered "pathetic boy" through the cat. He died when Lug blew the cat up in the atmosphere.

**Lord Cookie Mill** (also "Cookie Milk"): Agatha's familiar, a Sphinx of Wonder in cat form.
- Can fly. Its eyes go shark-black, and it uses Beguiling Defense to reflect damage.
- Rolled two natural 20 Strength saves.
- Exploded like a firework.

**Jimothy**: a deformed raccoon with a nub tail and mangled teeth, living in an asteroid crater in the Ether Drifts. "Jimothy goes where Jimothy wants." He might have blessed someone kind. Shooed and struck with a broom, he urine-sprayed both champions (acid) and fled.

**[[Characters/Thea|Thea]]**: the worm imp. She was stuck as a pink T-Rex after a polymorph from a knockoff wand out of the Box of Chaos, and wrecked the fortress hallway. The Arbiter restored her.

**[[Characters/Stuart|Stuart]]**: blamed for the tech failure. The Arbiter wants him fired; the Grandmaster offers him a bonus if the show survives. Whether he is in-world is unclear.

**[[Characters/The Arbiter|The Arbiter]]** (Drew): fixed Thea, and denies responsibility for the Box of Chaos ("Nobody's in charge of the box"). Wears Italian leather shoes. Enjoys toffee and Agatha's kindness. White glowing wings.

**[[Characters/Grandmaster Elusia Skypiercer|Grandmaster Elusia Skypiercer]]** (Bec): angry about the fortress damage and wants the box controlled. Switches sides repeatedly. Flustered by the ending, she crowned both champions.

**The worm civilization**: tiny multicolored worm-like beings underground in the asteroid crater. Agatha's fall crushed 60 of them. (Dried Blood: worm lore since about Season 1; their mayor was killed.)
