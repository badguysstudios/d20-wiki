A gnome pool caretaker in floaties and a drawn-on mustache met a velvet-voiced necromancer who was more or less Halloween in human form, and the Void's graveyard arena has not been the same since. **Squiggles** and **[[Ray S'urdead]]** fought among the dead while the Grandmaster blushed at one champion and begged him to destroy the other. It ended with a robbed Box of Chaos, an artificial sun, and fifty-eight furious ghosts proving that "I'm rubber, you're glue" is a valid combat strategy.

## The Story

### Trouble at the Front Door

The evening opened at the front door of [[The Dread Fortress]], a heavy wooden door bound in metal. A small pink [[Thea]] was dragging a giant orange pumpkin basket while [[Chinchillas|chinchillas]] pushed from the other side. It was the Halloween basket, she explained, full of goodies for trick-or-treaters. [[The Arbiter]] didn't care about the basket. He cared that the chinchillas were on their break.

The labor dispute that followed went badly for management. The chinchillas had been expecting overtime, because Thea had promised them triple pay. Thea pointed out that she couldn't read, so she couldn't know how to contract. She also couldn't take the promise back: "You can't take words and put them back in your mouth. I tried, because then I thought I will never be hungry again." She declared triple pay for everyone. The Arbiter shrugged it off. [[Stuart]], whose prank had apparently gotten the Arbiter "real good" earlier, had just had his pay docked, so the Arbiter would simply shift some numbers around.

When Thea demanded help, the Arbiter snapped his fingers. His eight coloured ring gems glowed and the basket floated into the air. Thea called it cheating and complained that he wasn't always "Mr. Approachable." He reminded her he had given her consciousness and existence itself, and explained that he looks straight over her because he has "a neck thing. I can't look down at people that are beneath me." With a clap he tore reality open and moved everyone, basket included, to the Grandmaster's throne room. Thea had only just got it to the door, and she began shedding hair in sheer stress. (Yes, Thea has hair.) The Arbiter snapped the basket back to the door and reminded everyone what night it was: not Prank Day, as Thea had hoped, but Death Day.

### Contracts, Quills and Crushes

The Arbiter ripped open a portal and presented the champions to [[Grandmaster Elusia Skypiercer|the Grandmaster]], who asked whether this was the prank. Squiggles insisted his mom really had named him Squiggles, though he admitted the mustache was drawn on. He had been floating in a pool moments earlier and had no idea why he was here. He had never received a welcome packet, because Thea had eaten it. She squatted and produced the contract the hard way. "We keep our pets hydrated," the Grandmaster noted.

Ray was a study in disgust. When Squiggles tugged his jacket for a pen, Ray batted him away with perfectly manicured nails: "Do not touch me. Small thing." Squiggles countered that he was "actually pretty tall for my height." For a pen, the Arbiter shook Thea by the face until a pin fell out of her. Ray had other ideas. He drew a feather quill, jabbed Thea gently in the backside, and signed in her blood, which turned out to be teal. The Grandmaster explained that Thea's blood changes with her recent diet. Ray tasted a drop from the quill ("Delectable") and told a horrified Thea he had no interest in eating her, though he "would have use for your corpse." She assured him she was a terrible corpse.

Squiggles, borrowing the quill, grabbed Ray's hand with both of his wet ones. Ray wiped his hands on his jacket as if he had touched something unspeakable. When Squiggles mentioned he had already died about four times and somehow come back, Ray promised, "Well, I intend to bring you back. Trust me." Then he shook hands with a summoned shadow hand, which was freezing. "Death is rarely warm."

The Grandmaster offered the winner one wish. She was not coping well. Pulling the Arbiter aside, she confessed that Ray reminded her of the romance characters in the books hidden in a bookshelf in her back wall. "It sounds like he's in an audio book." When Ray purred that surely she had wishes of her own, her face flushed red. Staring at the ground, she announced, "Squiggles, I'm choosing you."

### Into the Graveyard

The Arbiter stood up from a chair nobody had seen him sitting in, which began the night's running gag, and portalled everyone into a cold, muggy graveyard. The Grandmaster warned [[The Acolytes]] not to look too closely and promised one champion would end up six feet under. The clouds parted, the moon came out, and Ray strolled in like a man arriving at a spa.

Squiggles admitted he had never read the contract; he never reads terms and conditions. He did work out Ray's name ("raise your dead"), which Ray called the first intelligent thing "the small mermaid" had said. Squiggles was thrilled: "Mermaid? Oh my gosh, that's an upgrade. Most people just call me, like, stupid."

His weapon of choice was a light hammer he keeps at work in case of kraken attacks, since krakens were what had eaten him those four times. His first bop was "like a child's toy hit," and Ray caught it in his palm. Then Squiggles launched into a spinning leap "like a Super Mario spiral through the air" and left a scuff on Ray's flawless cheek. The crowd erupted into chants of "Squiggles!" and the cheering hurt Ray more than the hammer did. "People like me," marvelled Squiggles, whose last boss had been a monkey who ridiculed him.

Ray, whose family of necromancers the Grandmaster dismissed as "a family of edgelords," answered with a ghastly illusion: every bone in his body shattering into a snapping pile of gore. Squiggles barely flinched. Ray reassembled and found the gnome unnervingly strong-willed. Looking closer, he saw "something dead behind those eyes… Something cold and black and lifeless." The eyes in question were muddy brown.

### The Bladder Incident

The Acolytes chanted for chaos and got it. All that hydration had everyone's bladders jiggling, and everyone, champions and crowd alike, had to hold it. The champions managed. In the stands, Gator, August and GF Powers did not, while Kruega held firm and earned a fortified blue slushie. Enough Acolytes failed that the whole arena reeked of piss and stung everyone but Kruega. Ray pressed a silk cloth to his face, Thea mopped the grass ("I'm working as hard as I can!"), and a chinchilla strolled onto the field to flag the safety violation. It promised to look the other way, provided it was fixed by the time it came back off its break.

### Oogie Boogie and the Shoelace

Squiggles revealed his wish: the missing right foot from his 1999 McDonald's Inspector Gadget Happy Meal toy collection. The Arbiter knew a guy. With his life suddenly important, Squiggles healed his piss damage ("Squiggles has a boo-boo, everyone") and ran to hide in a mausoleum. As he fled, Ray made finger guns, aimed with a big smile, and fired his own index finger as a giant bone bullet. Squiggles dodged it completely, because he had bent down to tie his shoelace. Yes, he wears socks and shoes in the pool, and no, they don't match.

Teej described Ray as "a physical embodiment of the holiday of Halloween," with jack-o'-lantern pendants on his jacket and a cloud of tiny useless ghosts trailing him. His necromancy only raises Halloween icons, so out of a nearby grave climbed a zombie Oogie Boogie Man. It danced over to the mausoleum singing its song with the wrong lyrics and missed every slam. "You pulled out the wrong body," the Grandmaster observed. Squiggles shimmied past it and told it to memorize the words next time. Then he drew a longsword, charged, and struck Ray with holy fury, followed by another hammer spiral. "I am unstoppable."

The roaring crowd gave both champions a surge. Ray spent his shrinking Squiggles' hammer to toddler-toy size, and Squiggles dropped it. Ray wrapped himself in stolen life, drawing aqua smoke from every grave into his chest, then flooded the arena with shadow. When it cleared, Squiggles had barely been touched and was wearing kids' sunglasses "you find at a Kmart." Oogie Boogie finally landed a slam, but Squiggles was still healthier than when he started.

### Let There Be Daylight

After the break, the Grandmaster shared popcorn with [[Doug the Pug|Doug]], and the Arbiter digitized the rest and sent it "through the internet" to the champions. Squiggles then wondered whether zombies melt in sunlight and tore open the sky, putting a sun over the arena and a second pair of sunglasses on his face. "I feel like I've really misunderstood Squiggles if he can rip open the sky and move the sun into this arena," said the Grandmaster. Oogie Boogie's sack merely singed, and it wiggled at him. Squiggles somersaulted away. Ray, his hair out of place and his jacket torn, found the "baby somersaults" almost insulting. "He's breaking," said the Arbiter. Squiggles declared, "Fuck it, we ball!" and poured every ounce of his healing touch into himself, swelling far beyond his normal health. "This has backfired stupendously," said the Grandmaster.

### The Box of Chaos

"I gotta give Ray a chance here," the Arbiter announced. Lightning struck his raised hands until he brought down [[The Box of Chaos]]. Pink and purple flames shot into the sun, a shadowy figure crawled out, and the box landed squarely on Oogie Boogie and crushed him. [[The Box of Chaos|Gerald]] slurped down one of the worms and admitted he could eat. Ray found a small headstone for a "loving Chihuahua companion," raised an undead Chihuahua named Pixie, and offered it up. Without breaking eye contact, Gerald took the dog and slowly lowered the lid. There was rumbling and barking. What happened to Pixie remains undisclosed.

The box reopened with a little fur on its teeth and swirled its flame around the stadium. It came to rest in Ray's hand as a single fireball he could throw whenever he liked. "This is energy and life. It is a new sensation." He tucked it into a jack-o'-lantern locket on his jacket. Then he peered into Squiggles' mind and found an abyss that stared back and smelled his fear. The gnome's actual surface thought was "Oh, look, a penny!" Shaken, Ray warned the hosts that "there is something inside that tiny creature." The Arbiter stood up, checked for himself, and agreed: "That guy's fucked up."

A curse meant to make Squiggles forget everything he knew bounced off; Squiggles was mostly worried about missing Antiques Roadshow. When the Grandmaster mocked him, Ray locked eyes with her and, without looking away, hurled the fireball. The Acolytes screamed his name, mostly out of frustration, and the blast hit Squiggles hard. Ray smirked at the Grandmaster against a backdrop of flame. "I am but a witness to this moment of greatness for you," she said.

### Robbing the Box

Squiggles' answer was to flip the Box of Chaos over and climb half inside. Gerald was lounging in a hammock. "Sorry, do you need something?" Squiggles rummaged past an outdated can of spam and nearly made off with a tome bearing a desiccated tongue on its cover before Gerald snatched it back: "You can't have that one." The Arbiter was stunned: "I don't think anyone's ever stolen from the box before." Squiggles came out with a signet ring of spell turning, which he wore like a bracelet. Then he healed himself again while sipping a juice box. "I am defeating death."

The Grandmaster dropped all pretense. Looking at Ray's feet, she begged him to have something in his back pocket. "For the love of God, destroy Squiggles." ("Hey! I can hear you… I thought we were cool!") Wanting, for the first time in a long time, to feel impressive, Ray called on every spirit in the graveyard. Fifty-eight answered.

### The Final Blow

Squiggles' ring flared and he rolled a perfect 20. The spirits, whose rest a necromancer had just stolen, turned around. Every one of them tore into Ray. Squiggles took off his sunglasses, wiped his eyes and told the spirits they could find peace as they bounced off him toward their summoner. When the cloud of fifty-eight tiny, angry faces dissipated, nothing was left of Ray but half a jack-o'-lantern locket.

The Grandmaster descended, picked up the half-locket, and buried it a few inches down. "A pretty face like yours could never exist in an arena like this." She stepped over the mound and told Squiggles she had always rooted for him and never wavered. His foot would be waiting in a package in the green screen room. When he clasped her hand with both of his, she gripped back with a death grip and dragged him into the sky, his floaties now completely dry. "A grand champion who shows that even the most unexpected winners can pull through. If he can do it, so can you." The Arbiter stood up.

## Dried Blood

Ray, back in voice, addressed those who had hoped to see him win: no one wanted it more than he did, but "a real victory is achieved in death, where we all will meet again very soon." Squiggles used his time for a meditation on a rubber duck. He had been haunted all his little life by a scene in a Harry Potter film where the Weasley dad asks Harry what a rubber duck is for. He knew it is technically a floating bath toy for children, and he refused to believe that was all it could be.
