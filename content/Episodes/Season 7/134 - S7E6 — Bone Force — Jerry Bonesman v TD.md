On Father's Day in [[The Void]], a training construct called [[TD]] and a vengeful skeleton pugilist named [[Jerry Bonesman]] fought in the sleepy, duck-filled [[The Duck Village|Duck Village]]. TD treated the whole match as a lesson. He stacked protective layers like a metallic onion and sealed himself inside an unbreakable dome. Jerry punched it, gnawed it, wrote math on a notepad about it, and turned [[Thea]] into a T-Rex to bite it. Then he woke the giant orc skull outside [[The Dread Fortress]], the first time anyone had seen the fortress speak, and the fight ended in one of the season's oddest and most tender deaths.

## The Story

### Father's Day in the Secret Lab
Deep inside the Dread Fortress, in an open lab strewn with cadavers, Thea hopped from body to body looting organs. She took hearts, livers, a few gallbladders and long strings of intestine. She tied them into a bouquet ("Intestines!… The army's gonna love this!") just as [[The Arbiter]]'s Italian leather shoes, "probably fake," came clicking down the stone. "This is my secret lab," he protested. "No one's supposed to know about this place."

Thea presented the falling-apart bouquet: "Happy Father's Day!" The Arbiter refused the title. He called her "a mindless worm that I plucked from the soil to do my bidding" and their arrangement "a business relationship." Thea pointed out that some people call him daddy. When he asked whether she had been reading his mail, she said, "You know I'm illiterate, so that's not true. But I have been opening it!" They settled on "Papa." He told her not to ruin the cadaver he was still using, then clapped them both into the throne room.

### The Bastion of Sorrow
[[Grandmaster Elusia Skypiercer|The Grandmaster]] greeted him with "Happy Father's Day, Arbiter." Everyone had heard him call himself daddy, she said, and opening his mail is "not a federal offense here in the Void." Thea strained every muscle to summon her two favourite [[Champions|champions]]. She let out a small fart that grew into a gaseous green portal full of glitter.

TD stepped through first. He was a rigid construct who moved more gracefully than expected, with metallic-sheened skin, vivid artificial blue eyes, hovering blue crystals and a cape that clearly wasn't for fashion. "This is not the training ground," he observed. "But there's so much training to be had upon these grounds." The Grandmaster was charmed. She suggested that if he lost, they might keep him as "the most entrancing art installation here."

Jerry came next, and he had to correct Thea at once: "It's Jerry Bones Man, all right? Not Bones Min." He was a polished white skeleton in glowing purple and cobalt robes, with ossified horns and a necrotic mist of tiny grasping hands that followed him everywhere. He made a poor first impression by asking whether the place was called "the Dread Shack or like the Dread Hut," and by complaining about the lack of bones in the decor. TD called the fortress "a bastion of sorrow." The Arbiter said that was exactly the vibe. The Grandmaster declared TD her chosen champion then and there, and promised the winner a pizza buffet.

TD can't eat, so the Arbiter offered intestines instead. Thea fetched a single strand, which the Grandmaster helpfully noted runs about 25 feet. TD tied it around his waist like a karate belt and magicked the blood off his hands. Jerry promised to "throw some hands… or maybe throw some bones. If I could wink, I would." He has no eyes. TD's verdict: "That sounds like a skill issue to me."

### Duck Village and the Layers
The Arbiter folded space and set them down in Duck Village, a quaint place of market stalls, a church and ducks walking through the fountain. The Grandmaster said it had been "demoted" to that name; the Arbiter insisted it was the official one. Above them, millions of [[The Acolytes|Acolytes]] had brought their fathers along and were hurling blue slushies at each other. The Grandmaster sat with popcorn and [[Doug the Pug]] and issued one rule: "Please do not harm the baby ducks."

TD went first and spent the whole turn powering up. A glowing armour booted up over him with a startup chime, a magical ward rippled over that, and then he simply beckoned Jerry forward. "The test is to get through multiple layers," he explained. Jerry answered with a three-fingered evil eye ("Stare into my evil eye hole") and an armour of his own, his body ossifying further, his ribs sealing shut and his cheekbones spiking outward.

TD added a frozen aura and a glint of blade-turning magic. Jerry cocked a haymaker: "Behold! My fisticuffs!" It landed, and the cold armour bit straight back into his bones. It also only reached the ward underneath. His second punch missed, and his hand audibly shattered. "Help," Jerry told the camera. "What am I doing here?" During a water break, TD dipped a finger in his glass and tossed it aside. Jerry gulped his down, and it splashed out through every empty hole in his body onto his robes and between his legs, so it looked like he had peed himself.

### The Birthday Gator
An alligator with piercing yellow eyes burst from the fountain and sang "Happy Birthday" at everyone. Jerry shrugged it off. TD didn't, and the song dragged up a buried memory: a bluish-skinned, possibly draconic figure celebrating a birthday for the first time, "the one he was created for." Without realising it, TD began to sing along, beautifully enough that the Grandmaster snapped, "Fuck you and your angelic stupid voice."

### The Wall of Force
Shaking off the memory, TD announced that "the first test is complete." He pressed a button on his hand and enclosed himself in a dome of pure force. The Acolytes went wild for him and showered him in blue raspberry slushie, and TD told Jerry in Draconic, "Your turn. The next test, the breakthrough."

Jerry bared his arm. Ice crystals formed along it, and his bones cracked and oozed black ichor that hardened into a Dread Hand. He dedicated the next blow to every level-one skeleton ever slain by adventurers: "Too many of my skeleton brothers are dead." "This is the revenge story I didn't know we needed," said the Grandmaster. The fingers lashed at the dome like tentacles and did nothing. The crowd loved it anyway, stripping off their clothes and brawling.

Thea brought Jerry a notepad and pencil: "It'll help you write down your thoughts." TD offered riddles about little Tommy's apples. Jerry asked if he could just push the wall. The Grandmaster summed it up: "Jerry is all bones, no brain." TD pressed a hand to the dome, Titanic-style, and told him, "You will learn eventually." Then he added necrotic protection and settled in to dodge. "This is like fighting a Russian nesting doll," said the Arbiter.

### The Box and the T-Rex
The Arbiter couldn't stand to watch any longer. A bolt of multicoloured lightning brought [[The Box of Chaos]] slamming down at Jerry's feet, wreathed in yellow and pink fire, with a shadowy figure leaning over its side. When Jerry called it "Mr. Box," it replied, "Mr. Box was my father. He's dead now." Jerry started to wish his late father a happy Father's Day, and the Box told him to shut the fuck up.

The Box would "certainly not" put a brain in his skull, but it would take bones ("I'm building something"). Jerry handed over his shattered wrist and hand. "This is gonna come in handy," said the Box, and the Grandmaster refused to let the pun go. In exchange it gave him a large animal femur that worked as a wand of polymorph. Jerry tried his evil eye through the dome again. It stung, but TD kept the wall up.

Thea then volunteered as tribute. She told everyone about the fortress's "good dental program" and her enormous Tooth Fairy earnings, which made Jerry realise he could have been running that con for years. TD tried to stop the transformation as a teaching moment, "a each one teach one moment," but the spell went through. Thea became a massive pink T-Rex with bulging eyes and threw her whole body at the dome. It held. Before the break, the Arbiter stated his only requirement: "As long as one of you ends up dead, I'm gonna be happy." TD replied: "Noted. Switching to obliteration mode."

### Obliteration Mode
After the break, an exhausted Thea Rex napped by the fountain, and the village had somehow grown into a town ("The sign has changed"). TD drew energy out of the dome, which pulsed like a beating heart, to heal himself and rebuild his ward. When his eyes reopened, they were red.

Jerry thanked Thea for "believing in the bones." Then he asked about the giant skull he had seen outside the fortress. It belonged to the Grandmaster, she said, and "no one has ever asked its name before." The name was Seavold. Chanting "Seavold, be bold, be bold, Jerry, be bold, be the bone," Jerry channelled his bone lord Bonar and the skull together. True horns erupted from his head and spines jutted from his back: "Now I am the great bones man." The skull's call broke reality, and both champions landed on the front lawn of the Dread Fortress among endless lava fields, with the orc skull's eyes beginning to glow.

Jerry burst into bone dust and reformed inside the dome, face to face, because "he's not going to bone from behind." TD copied the trick and stepped out, leaving Jerry sealed inside. "You still have not learned, bone man," he said, and soon after, "Only one has ever beat me." "That's a fucking masterclass," said the Grandmaster. A true third eye socket split open in Jerry's forehead, and as his hex took hold, strange flashes appeared that might have been Seavold's history. TD filled the dome with psychic static, "a whole disco laser show." When the smoke cleared, a bony hand waved it away: "I'm still here. I'm still here."

TD dropped the wall. Jerry, who had been planning to dig his way out, demanded he put it back. "Does not compute," said TD. Jerry tried "please." Charging in with a high-pitched scream, he cursed TD and found a new lesson: "punching that wall of force taught me how to punch back with force." TD was frightened at last: "Egads, I am shaking in my bones." In reply, TD made Jerry see training denizens like himself beaten down by warriors chasing their own success. The Grandmaster named the agony they shared and ruled that every hit would now do double damage. Jerry rolled a natural 20 to resist and wondered, "Have I become the thing I was fighting against?" He held TD with his fist cocked: "If one of us gets out of this alive, we can make a change." "Perhaps we can," said TD.

### Seavold Speaks
In round eight, a round the show didn't even have placards for, TD split into mirror images. They caught Jerry's fists, steered the blows into their own faces and chests, and shattered. Jerry admitted he "has no utility" and prayed to the skull. Its eyes lit, a jaw rose out of the ground, and Seavold spoke. Jerry offered to be its knight, defending all undead and "all skeletons, even the ones inside of the people that have flesh still." Seavold sneered, "You are but an ant to me." TD stood up for his opponent, and Seavold called the match "a farce." Then, before Acolytes who had never seen the Dread Fortress move, the giant head attacked them both with every insult it knew: "Training dummy, skeleton fodder, bone dust, calculator." TD's ward shattered.

### Training Complete
The Arbiter stood and pulled out his metal chair:

> **The Arbiter:** "If one of you guys doesn't die, I'm gonna come down there and fucking kill you both."

All damage was now tripled. TD drove a psychic lance into Jerry's skull while Jerry led the crowd in a "Bonesman!" chant. The Grandmaster asked what he saw, now that he could finally calculate faster than anyone and death was at his door. Jerry ran the numbers one last time and found peace: TD was the right one, he would stop this from happening again, and "sometimes somebody else has to take the torch."

At the last moment a bony hand and a metal one clasped, "the skeletons and the dummies of the world." Jerry crumbled to dust from the inside out, and his hand turned to dust as it fell. "Training complete," said TD. A slight voice whispered behind him, "You trained him well." "And this is just phase one," TD replied, as green crept into his blue eyes: his second directive was necromancy. The Grandmaster offered him the pizza he couldn't eat. He chose the intestines as his topping, and she raised his hand to the Acolytes: "Let the training continue."

## Dried Blood
Afterward, the players looked back on how badly matched the two had been. Kyle said the Wall of Force saga "felt like five episodes of Dragon Ball Z," and admitted Jerry had been planning to cartoon-shovel his way under the dome. Kyle, as the show's "bone salesman," made a closing pitch: "Hey, you got broken bones? We got new ones for you right here." The Arbiter and the Grandmaster realised they now owed the giant orc skull a backstory. "We just liked his skull," the Grandmaster shrugged. "We're like snails… or hermit crabs." TD's parting creed: "The training does not end when the adventure begins."
