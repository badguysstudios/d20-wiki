On the one day off a year the unions had won her, [[Thea]] stole a car, ran down a good part of a candy town, found her long-lost little brother inside a badly built robot, and tried to murder her boss. The episode was a one-shot, "Thea's Revenge," and it finally told her story. She was a worm of the Hollow, sent to kill [[The Arbiter]] under a blood oath. A god had helped her get the job, and she had mostly forgotten all of it. By the end, one candy kingdom had been vaporized twice, one champion had been delivered by portal in his swim trunks to be blown up, and Thea had been promoted to ambassador, with no raise and a pope hat.

## The Story

### A Mission in the Dark

Far from [[The Dread Fortress]], past [[Prexmarin]] and deep in an asteroid belt, a seven-foot warforged knelt before a council of figures hidden in darkness. "Do you understand your mission?" "Of course." It wore tattered clothes, leathery gauntlets and boots, and carried a shield and an obnoxiously large longsword that spat little sparks. It stood up and walked backwards into the shadows without ever breaking eye contact with the council, then backed straight into a portal.

### Thea's Day Off

Back at the Fortress, the Arbiter clacked his Italian leather boots up to Thea's room, which is a closet, and knocked. Thea was overjoyed: "I have a door? … Look at this door!" He had installed it so she would stay in her room at night. "Who's there?" "It's me." "I don't know me." He had come to tell her it was her day off. Ever since the goblins and the [[Chinchillas|chinchillas]] unionized, the bylaws gave everyone in the Dread Fortress one day off a year. Thea asked whether today was the day she cleaned his shoes. No, that was tomorrow.

She wanted to go to the beach, or eat lots of candy, or go to the bathroom, preferably the bathroom first. "Listen, it's your day off," said the Arbiter. "I honestly don't give a shit what you do." Thea slid out into the hallway: "I don't have to give a shit anymore. I'm good to go." He asked whether she had just taken a shit in her room. "It was my room. No one said your room couldn't be a bathroom." He had to admit nobody had ever said that. Thea pressed the point: she had a throne, and the [[Grandmaster Elusia Skypiercer|Grandmaster]] had a throne, so both of them outranked him. He gave up. They would go through a portal together, he would run his errands, and they would meet back at six o'clock. He snapped a watch onto her wrist, letting her choose, and she chose a giant watch of pure diamond. He had expected a Casio.

### Sugar Hills

The portal opened onto [[Sugar Hills]], a vast land made entirely of candy. Thea started drooling out of every orifice. "It looks like Candyland! But it has the name of, like, a white woman's home in a suburban landscape." To everyone's shock she knew everything about it: it lies across the Void, it is a huge marketplace for many kinds of people, the underground candy market runs through it, and people come for a good time. Everything is lickable, and the streets are icing. She did a happy tippy-tap dance in it. The Arbiter, she noticed, had put hospital shoe covers over his boots.

He reminded her that it was her day off and she didn't have to work. Then he handed her a list of things to fetch for him, which was "totally not work." Thea couldn't read it, but she called it a scavenger hunt. He walked off, and she set out with no money and her tongue dragging along the ground, licking the street as she went, while candy music played from every corner.

Her first stop was a hot chocolate stand. A large man stood behind it, wiping the same spot on the counter, under a sign she couldn't read: **[[Emmery]]'s Number Two Hot Chocolate**. Emmery had franchised. He read the list for her. The first item was a driveshaft. He didn't sell those, but he did have a car, so Thea made an offer: "Come here, driveshift! Or else I'll break all your bones and then stick them into the icing and light them on fire and make my own damn birthday cake out of them!" Emmery handed over his keys. "Just don't go back."

The car was a 1994 Pontiac Bonneville. "It's one you would give a very stupid teenager in hopes that they won't rack up your insurance. I love it!" There was a paved road behind the shops. Thea drove down the candy sidewalks instead, with her tires sticking in the icing and townsfolk diving for their lives. An old man complained that he had been walking there. "Not anymore, old fartso." He fell, couldn't get up because his life alert got no reception, and slowly sank into the icing and drowned.

She stopped at a roadside stall run by a very tall, slender, very good-looking man in fine leathers. His belt was covered in knives, and he sold candy canes sucked down into shivs. He read her list too, and the next item was forty sharpened candy canes. He happened to have exactly forty. Since Thea didn't know how to open a trunk, he broke into it with a knife and loaded them for her. "If you need any more knives, you tell your friends that [[Baptiste]] sent you." "I will never remember that name ever again. Thank you, you very weird man, and goodbye forever!" She drove off without paying. When he shouted after her, she closed her ear flaps.

### Have You Seen This Dick with a Pube?

Elsewhere in Sugar Hills, the warforged stepped out of its portal knee-deep in candy sludge, lamenting how long its boots would take to clean. It found the most respectable-looking man around and unrolled a huge scroll with its own drawing of the person it was hunting: a line with two eyes and a smirk, labelled "WORM," spelled W-Y-E-M. The man checked his pocket watch. "What I'm seeing here is a dick with a pube. You're asking me if I've seen a dick with a pube?" The warforged allowed that perhaps that was how they identified the individual on this planet. "Have you seen this dick with a pube?" "I've seen a lot of dicks around here, but not one like that." "I wasn't asking about your sexuality." The man sent it to the food vendors, and it left the way it did everything, walking backwards and staring.

It tripped backwards into a candied hot dog stand, where a large man named **[[Frank]]** was stirring boiling sugar with a giant wooden spoon and smoking over the pot. "You can candy anything, my guy." Pencils? Car keys? A chair? A ladder? Then the warforged reached down without looking, picked up a kobold by the neck and asked if Frank could candy *this*. Frank had standards. "Like, morally, I like… Well, it depends. Are you rich?" "I'm rich in spirit and in character." "Oh, you're just another bum. Get out of here!" The kobold was set down unharmed. Frank candied the scroll instead, plunging his own hands into the sugar, and when the warforged leaned in obnoxiously close to whisper, "Have you seen a worm, perhaps?", he pointed it to the gummy worm stand. The backwards walking, it turned out, was not a choice. Its pilot simply had terrible control of the suit and could not turn around.

### Little Brother

Thea came barrelling down the road with her eyes closed, singing, and clipped the warforged with the Bonneville. It went face down in the sugar. She offered to pull it out if it would read her list, sealed the deal with a pinky promise (a backwards claw sticking out of the icing), dropped into a deep Thea squat and heaved. Behind them, the abandoned car kept rolling through town. Pretty much everyone the warforged had talked to got hit by it, and far off Frank cried, "Oh, my knee." Thea called it a strike.

Then she looked into its face. "Those eyeballs. I know those balls from anywhere. Theo?" The head and chest popped open. "Thea, it's me!" "Give me a hug, little brother!" Inside the suit was **[[Theo]]**, a worm with bulbous eyes and no arms. The worms had built the mech so his wiggling could work it, but they had no thumbs or hands, so it looked impressive and wasn't any good. He was startled by her new body and especially her four hands. She explained they were "a bit of a curse and kind of a gift," given by the Arbiter, and that she'd keep them because they're handy for doors.

Theo had been searching for eleven years, five months, thirty-something days and twenty-seven seconds. He had spent seven months stuck in a vending machine he mistook for the Fortress. Thea admitted she had once been stuck in a vending machine for about ten years, so it probably smelled like her. He had also fought a pigeon for thirty-seven days. Thea went grave: "What was its name? Sean…" "I fucking hate Sean." "I fucking hate Sean, too. He's such an asshole." Sean had kept trying to eat her. She gave Theo a candy-cane shiv from the trunk, and they walked back along a road now lined with bodies, broken stalls and burning shops. "Well, if you need any body parts, I've sourced many right now for you."

After a secret tongue shake to prove she could still be trusted, Theo asked the real question: had she defected from the worms? "I'm still a worm. I will always be a worm. Thea is a worm forever and ever… Always remember the Hollow." He had come to bring her home to debrief. Thea couldn't leave: she was doing both jobs, the undercover one and this one, which she took very seriously so she could keep it. Theo told her the truth: "I miss my sister, and they say they're afraid that you've switched sides." She hadn't, she insisted. It was complicated, and she had forgotten some things. She'd grown fond of the Deathmatches too. Worms have no bones, and everyone at the Hollow had always been fascinated with the breaking of bones. "I haven't broken the bones of who I was supposed to break the bones of yet. … I took a blood oath that I would do it."

Then Thea had an idea. Maybe she didn't keep her job. Maybe she went for a promotion. "If I kill the Arbiter, then not only do I kill him and fulfill my promise to the Hollow, but then I also get to have a new job, and then I can be the Arbiter! … I can hire you, and I can hire all the other worms that were smashed to smithereens, and I'll finally get to avenge Mayor Timothy!" "Mayor Timothy!" Welcome, the Arbiter announced, to Thea's revenge.

### The Gummy Gnome Army

The Arbiter wasn't due back until six, and Thea remembered he had gone to "a cave in the opposite direction." So they planned a trap: something sticky, something sweet, something sour, and an army of gummy gnomes with brains put inside them, Frankenstein-style. The gummy worm stand turned out to be a crater like everything else on the street. There were gummies everywhere, people crying, children wailing, and townsfolk who had been run over bleeding into the candy road. There were gummy gnomes all over the ground, and real gnomes too, though Thea only wanted the gummies. She paid in candy canes. The Bonneville, half-parked in a restaurant and caked in candy, blood and viscera, started right back up, and they packed forty-nine gummy gnomes into the trunk.

Theo rode with his head popped open and took charge of the radio. He picked classical; it played slow jams. "Somebody die?" "Someone's about to." He told her the worms had started building the mech suits not long after she left, and he had volunteered because he missed her. Everyone had been sad for a while, but now there was determination against the Arbiter. Thea wondered whether they had lost faith in her, then reminded him she was indestructible. She had drunk a whole potion as a kid and glowed silvery for twenty minutes, so the Arbiter only *thinks* he has revived her all those times. "That dumb idiot." She asked whether he could still do the family secret, the **Thermopolis**. Theo was more than prepared to deploy it.

Theo had also learned on other worlds that people have last names, sometimes several. Thea demanded one. Was the price blood? One of her hands? He explained that a last name is given by someone who loves you, like family. She wanted one of *his*, he wouldn't give them up, and they wrestled until her foot-hand was in his face and the car hit a giant candy cane tree. The Bonneville was too old for airbags, so both went through the windshield into the sludge. They looked up at a giant gingerbread castle with cupcake trim at the end of the road. The car burned behind them.

The gnomes were melting. Theo suggested they let them melt into one mega gnome, so they squished the forty-nine into four giant ones that looked like a worm in a mech suit had made them. Thea insisted on tails, horns and buttholes: "Well, if something eats… then it has to have an exit. It's only kind." This was a dessert place, so she gave them big cake butts too.

### The Worms Inside Your Body

The crash, the explosion and the screaming about buttholes brought the Arbiter running out onto the castle's drawbridge. "Thea?" "Thea's not available right now. Please leave a message after the beep. Beep!" He had been handling some diplomatic business. He had given her a fake list just to keep her busy on her day off. That was the last straw. Thea listed her duties: "Picking up poop, eating poop, shoveling poop out of the way, picking up dead bodies, burying dead bodies, un-burying dead bodies, putting the bodies at the table to scare the Grandmaster." "But you like poop," he said. "You pooped in your own room."

"We have a gift for you. It's the gift of death. Theo, get him!" Theo's voice box had been wrecked in the crash, so he advanced backwards with his sword raised, rolled a perfect search, and still kept missing the Arbiter, who lazily sidestepped out of his peripheral vision until Theo walked past him. Theo announced his full name, Theo Thracklebolt Thermompolus Thuundar Thed, but the broken voice box turned it to static and the Arbiter caught about 30% of it. Thea spelled it out: "We are the Worms from the Hollow. We are here to avenge Mayor Timothy." The Arbiter didn't remember any [[Timothy]]. His eyes flickered with lightning as he reversed time inside his own head. "Oh, that guy?"

"He was a hero! We all voted for him! He was making changes! He built a playground! We loved him!" The Arbiter pointed out that they had sent a gift basket. Thea was having none of it. A few measly cheeses would not satisfy the worms. They had plotted their revenge for years ("and by years I mean probably more like minutes"). They had sent Thea, then she forgot what she was doing, so they sent Theo. "You thought you were a puppet master. But no, we are the worms inside your body puppeting you."

### A Deal with Fil

Theo's heel thrusters fired like little Iron Man boosters and his sword came down hard. It bounced off the Arbiter's rippling muscles. Theo swung again at those beloved Italian leather shoes, and the Arbiter whipped his foot out of the way: "Hey, hey, hey, don't make this personal." Theo said he had made it personal by convincing his sister to stay. "I just picked a random worm and turned it into my servant," the Arbiter said.

"You thought you did," said Thea. She had made an offering to the god **[[Fil]]**, and Fil had given her control of that moment. "Oh, Fil, yeah, they're really cool. They just came down and were like, hey, for a handshake… I'm like, well, I don't have any hands because I'm a worm. And they're like, one day you will, and then you're going to give me a handshake." The Arbiter snapped his fingers, lifted her into the air and twisted her into a pretzel. "You fucking idiot! You've been talking to Fil!" Thea asked him to get a bit more on the right side because her shoulder had been killing her, and mocked the damage: "Even [[Richard Knottley]] can hit harder than that."

The Arbiter launched into a long lament. For years he had been looking for a servant smart enough for mediocre tasks but not smart enough to betray him, and good help was so hard to find. While he monologued, Thea tried to fit his whole head inside her mouth to suffocate him. He palmed her like a basketball and dribbled her without breaking stride. So she pulled a shiny silver pistol out of her pocket and threw it at him. Thea is a monster hunter who attacks with her intelligence, so she knew his weak spot and hit him square in the crotch. It did more damage than he had done to her. "Yeah, you get him, sis!"

Theo lit his sword on fire with a swipe of his metal hand and went for the shoes again. He missed, and the blade sank into the candy and hardened in place, sword-in-the-stone style. The Arbiter, who has advantage on stomping worms, stomped at him and missed, and his boot went straight through the chocolate-bar bridge into the syrup below. Theo used the lull to bend his flattened sister back into shape. "Remember, I'm indestructible." The Arbiter answered with finger guns to both their heads, which blasted them ten feet back. "You're never going to kill me, and I'm going to kill you, and then I have to start the whole job application process over again." Thea shrugged it off: "That's no worse than when Tom used to fart on us."

Thea pulled out another gun, the Baron's Blunderbuss, and threw that too. "You're supposed to shoot those." "I'll shoot what I need to shoot. Also, I don't know how to use that." The Arbiter told her to take it from someone who had led a successful coup that she didn't have what it takes. She vowed the whole Hollow would become the Arbiter, "except the Grandmaster. She seems kind of cool. I like her hair." The Grandmaster was in charge, he protested. Thea said he had led her astray. And anyway, he was the one who shit in the closets. "They call it a water closet!" He was done with this. He would not debase himself by fighting "you two peons." "Why are you peeing on us?" asked Theo.

### Lightning and the Pebble

The Arbiter raised a hand, and multicoloured chaos lightning struck it again and again until the crackling energy took the form of a person. Thea recognized it at once. When she was still a worm, crawling around the arena drinking up the blood, she had watched this champion: **[[Baron Voltágè]]**. "I channel the lightning!" The spectral Baron blasted Theo and then dissipated. "Thea, this worm, your brother, it's a bad influence. I'm going to free you of him."

"Two can play that game. You know who is resistant to lightning? Pebble." Thea stomped all four hands into the ground, Sugar Hills shook, and a former champion rose from the cracked earth: **[[The Pebble]]**. Thea had revived him with a potion, and he owed her one. "I will smash your face in and then we'll slip back through the cracks." He did exactly that.

Theo was smoking now, and cinders were popping out of the suit. "All systems are starting to go critical." He winked at his sister, which from outside looked like the helmet bobbing for no reason, and she bobbed back. Thea threw one more pistol ("another one") and missed, then offered terms. If the Arbiter surrendered, he could keep the pistol, and one of his Italian leather shoes. "I'm taking the other one," he said, and cackled into the sky.

### The Thermopolis

"Thea, it's time for the Thermopolis! … Hit the deck!" Theo charged at his burning sword and the cackling Arbiter at once, planning to blow the suit and leap out at the last second. He guided it in perfectly. Then he rolled a 1 trying to jump clear. Thea couldn't get clear either. The blast did three hundred and eleven points of damage and vaporized them both instantly. Thea's last words were "You killed us. This is horrible." Theo's were "I always wanted to play the violin!" ("He would have been terrible.")

It was Judgment Day for Sugar Hills. The candy melted and oozed, and for miles there was nothing left. In the middle of the wasteland stood the Arbiter, covered in soot and a little of his own blood, boots scuffed, wings on fire. He patted them out, and then the melted bridge dropped him into the syrup and filled his boots. "Thea, you could have been so helpful to me. It took me so long to find someone who had that kind of hatred and rage within them. Somebody who is that bent on killing me."

He reached up into the heavens and pulled. The clouds rolled backwards, the sun rose and set in reverse, and Thea and Theo were back where they had stood before the explosion, unharmed and fully remembering being vaporized. "I am the lord of all time and space. I feel like we've covered this."

### Revenge, Redirected

He tried diplomacy. Fine, they had been a little careless. They got Timothy killed; it happens. Sure, they had destroyed the worms' cities, and "that big door that one time." "Did I grind your sacred crystal into salt?" It was rhetorical. Theo answered anyway: yes, he did. "Yes, of course. I ate the salt. It was delicious. But… I didn't kill Timothy." He loved revenge, he told them ("I'm like the biggest revenge guy"), but theirs was aimed at the wrong guy. All he had done was suggest a game of tag. He didn't make anybody "grab your mayor and throw him like an exploding Krillin into the sky." Champions have their own agency. Then he snapped his fingers, and **[[Strako]]** fell out of a portal at the end of the bridge, wearing swim trunks from his own beach day. "What's going on, guys?" "Yeah, you want revenge. Listen, this is the guy."

Thea's history was, improbably, perfect. She remembered the scene exactly: Strako had asked nobody's permission. He had looked around for something living, grabbed it, tagged it and thrown it into space. So she negotiated. Her demands were Strako, at least twenty front-row seats at every Deathmatch, a ten percent cut of every match held over the Hollow, a pair of Italian leather shoes, and legs and hands for Theo so he could play the violin. Theo added front-row tickets to *Rent*.

The Arbiter, feeling generous and very diplomatic, granted the seats. He snapped his fingers and reshaped Theo into an imp like Thea, but with hands only and no arms, "just T-Rex hands." He threw in a violin, which Theo couldn't play because his hands were too close together. He agreed to pay royalties back to the Ether Drifts and the Hollow. Then he made a confession of his own: he had sensed her hatred long ago and always suspected she would try to betray him. "I just didn't think you had it in you. And that makes me proud." He named Thea the Dread Fortress's official ambassador to the worms of [[The Ether Drifts]], on top of all her current duties, with no extra pay and a second day off each year. "Do I get a hat? Like the Pope?" She could have a "Popat," he decided, but then the extra day off was cancelled. Final terms: one day off, two jobs, one Popat. "So, do we have a deal?"

"Yes. Thermopolize him, Theo!"

Theo sent the mech suit roaring at Strako and tried once more to jump clear. Just before the blast, the Arbiter threw a shield around himself and Thea. Strako stood blinking in his beachwear and saw the worm inside the glowing suit. In the briefest moment he understood, and he muttered, "I'm sorry." Then he and Theo were vaporized, and Sugar Hills was a melted wasteland once more. "At least he got to play the violin," said Thea.

A small scrap of burning paper floated down in front of her. With his new hands Theo had tried to write, and Thea snatched the note and held it to her chest. She can't read. "Those are words. He drew me nice words. Just like mama used to write." Beside the words was a little doodle of a line with two eyes and a smile: a worm, or possibly a few balls. She kept it secret. What it says is a story for another day.

"Are we good here now?" "Good enough for now." She had four giant gummy gnomes for him, and the chinchillas would love them. There wasn't room in the portal. Back in the Dread Fortress, the Arbiter had one more thing to say. "Maybe we don't mention any of this to the Grandmaster." "Mention what?" "Exactly." Thea sauntered off one way, and the Arbiter clacked off the other.

## Dried Blood

Thea, now a diplomat with an official hat, went home to report to the council. Her hat was tall, pope-shaped and a hot, vibrant blue. She brought a small crystal orb containing the ghost of her brother. ("Can you hear me?" "I'm awake." "I am dead.") She knocked with her third foot-hand. The council sat far above in the dark, with one harsh light on the speaker below, and **Old Todd**, who refers to himself in the third person, welcomed her back and wondered whether he should get a hat like that.

Old Todd hoped the Arbiter was finally dead and the Deathmatches over. Not exactly, said Thea. Theo had been "thermopolized," and the Arbiter had survived and rewound time, "which was really nice because then Theo lived." But they had found the real murderer of Mayor Timothy, Strako. They had also won front-row seats for life, "because we do love the bone crushing," and Thea could slip the worms the dead bodies, "because we do love to eat the dead bodies afterwards." Old Todd summed it up. Instead of killing the Arbiter, ending the Deathmatches and getting justice for Timothy, and for years of their civilization's destabilization, she had negotiated a truce "for a nobody and some front row tickets?"

Thea explained the big-brain version: the truth, a tourism boom, endless dead bodies for food, and Theo's new hands. Next, they would use Theo's death to negotiate for all the worms to get hands and arms. She would visit once a year on her vacation: "Send all letters to TheaIsGreat over at my email and I will answer them. If you have any other needs, don't." Theo asked for a holiday in his honor and a parade. Old Todd said they would discuss it. Theo waved his hands at the council on the way out: this could be you.

When the doors shut behind Thea and Theo, out of their earshot, Old Todd turned to the others. "Traitors. Handed traitors giving up the sleek, warm physique for the joys of handed pleasure." It was time, he said, for step 20 of Mayor Timothy's plan. He shouldered open a book, with some difficulty since he has no hands, and had to ask for help opening it. The page read: **Step 20: Call Fil.**

At the table, the creators said they had been holding Thea's story in their back pocket for a while, and that the worms had waited long enough. Thea also forgot to ask the Arbiter to rewind time for Theo, which everyone agreed was very on brand. The gun-throwing was RealLifeFakeWizard's idea, and Caustic Phoenix vowed that any character of hers with a gun will only ever throw it from now on. The walking backwards came from wanting Theo to be completely incompetent in the suit.
