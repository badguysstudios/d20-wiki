A tiefling who loves rocks met a doddering old wizard who turned out to know no magic at all. The arena around them became a sea of lava, then vanilla-flavored snow. **Coen** (played by seum) and **[[Alfucius the Everbright]]** (played by CantripBreakers) spent most of the match making friends, feeding a familiar to [[The Box of Chaos|Gerald]] and plotting behind a floating dragon skull. When the Grandmaster finally demanded blood, the deathmatch became a team-up against a ghost dragon. It ended in ballet, carnivorous tulips and one very unlucky goblin.

## The Story

### Fetch Day at the Fortress

The episode opened with [[The Arbiter|the Arbiter]] still in chains, serving his punishment for his failed coup. He clanked through the halls of [[The Dread Fortress|the Dread Fortress]] as slowly as possible, making a show of it so people would feel sorry for him. A strange noise drew him to an open courtyard. Inside, some odd magic had conjured a sunny day underground, and [[Grandmaster Elusia Skypiercer|the Grandmaster]] was crouched on the grass with [[Doug the Pug|Doug]] and a plushy twenty-sided die.

"Who's a good boy? Who wants the ball?" She threw it a heroic seven feet. Doug zoomed after it, tore it viciously back and forth, and trotted back. The Grandmaster had even frozen chicken broth into ice cubes for him because of the heat. The Arbiter was unmoved: "He doesn't need any snacks. Look how fat he is." All Doug did, he added, was "sit around and lick himself." When she claimed licking was exhausting and that he wished he had Doug's flexibility, he tried to prove otherwise and turned out to be exactly as flexible as you might expect.

He reminded her that "Tuesdays are the dog training days. You know that Sundays are the deathmatch days." She asked Doug whether he was ready for a deathmatch. Doug barked and ran in a circle, and with that approval she called for two grand champions. "I've got champions. I don't know how grand they are," the Arbiter replied. He clapped, and visible sound waves rippled off the walls and tore open a portal.

### A Tiefling and a Statue

**Coen** stepped through first. She was a very tall, lean tiefling with pale bluish-green skin, cloudy blue hair and horns hung with golden chains and charms. She wore a sleeveless green coat with dark feathers at the shoulders and had a long pointed tail. She was promptly distracted by crickets, and then by a snail. When the Grandmaster snapped at her, Coen just admired the atmosphere: "It's beautiful. It's nice and crisp."

Her opponent stood utterly still and answered everything with "Hey" or "Hi." "Did you forget to bring his voice into this plane as well?" the Grandmaster asked. Doug gnawed on his toe. She pushed him, and he didn't budge, like a London guard. Meanwhile Coen picked up a vaguely four-sided rock to roll for her starting position. When she tied, she hunted through the rubble for a matching palm-sized stone. "I have a thing for rocks," the Grandmaster declared, and blessed her for it.

Then the statue blinked awake from what he called a meditative state. He was a wizened old man in flowing purple robes, with a long white beard and a very pointed hat. He bowed deeply to the Grandmaster and introduced himself as Alfucius the Everbright. He claimed he had served alongside the librarians of Candlekeep, the Blackstaff of Waterdeep and the magic weavers of [[Strixhaven]] and Eberron. The Grandmaster took Coen's blessing straight back and handed it to him: "You like rocks, but he bowed to me." Alfucius then objected to the word "death," having assumed this was a talent show. "Whoever's more talented at murdering the other," the Grandmaster clarified. The Arbiter leaned over to Coen and suggested the old man might just die on his own. She agreed.

He nearly did. When Alfucius patronizingly asked about her "skill set, little one," Coen quietly tripped him. He fell flat on his face and broke his nose, so **blood was drawn before the match had even started**. "Oh, it's all right," he said, "he was crooked anyway." He then opened his cloak to release Blinkus Maximus, a tiny radiant construct like a beetle, built around a diamond. Alfucius called it his homunculus ("Sorry, my familiar") and said it had travelled the multiverse with him. The Arbiter spread his great white wings, shot into the sky and roared, "Acolytes, you have come for dungeons and dice and deathmatches. Well, let's fucking get started then."

### The Floor Is Lava

[[The Acolytes]] chanted for chaos. The Arbiter snapped his fingers and Gerald slammed down from the sky, cracking the stone in a column of pink and yellow flame, his yellow eyes peering out from the dark. The Grandmaster had planned to pace out the chaos. She changed her mind and set off two events at once.

First, the pebbles melted and **the ground became lava**. Both champions leapt onto tall rocks, facing each other across a glowing sea. Alfucius drew a long, beautiful wand: "I hope you know what it means to deal with a wizard, Coen." It produced sparks and noises and absolutely nothing else. As the light faded, he hiked his robes above his knees and fled across the rocks, singeing his legs when he fell short of one jump. The bones of a giant dragon rose to float in the lava. Gerald paddled his box through it like a canoe, singing his favorite Pocahontas song. As the Grandmaster pointed out at the table, the box is fireproof.

### Will You Be My Friend?

The second event cracked open the rock Alfucius had just abandoned like an egg. Out peeked **[[Dolly]]**, a small porcelain doll with raggedy hair and unblinking glass eyes. She held out her hand: "Will you be my friend?" Coen took it. Dolly burned a best-friend rune into her palm and became her familiar, growing fur over her torso and even bigger, glassier eyes. "You just had to do it, didn't you?" the Arbiter sighed. Coen renamed her **Aragorl**.

The rune's sting sent Coen and her new friend invisible. She blinked sixty feet in a burst of sparks and touched Alfucius with a crackling shock, and he lit up like a Christmas tree. She apologized at once: "I wanted to introduce you to someone." He stared at the doll. "I don't like that. I don't like that at all." He threw fifteen gold on the floor between them. Coen caught two coins and threw them back at him, insisting she already had "the most priceless gift." He proposed an alliance, and Dolly smiled wide enough to show every tooth. "I have one friend," said Coen.

Then Coen stopped to listen to the crickets for a full minute, in total silence. Alfucius used the pause to send Blinky sneaking toward the Box of Chaos, with a throat-slitting gesture that the Grandmaster briefly took as an order to assassinate Gerald. Then Aragorl bit him, gnawing in poison with her tiny teeth while he flubbed his chance to resist yet again. "Let it be known," the Grandmaster declared, "do not let ancient, demonically possessed dolls ever bite you."

More chaos followed. Rainless lightning crackled down on both champions. The Grandmaster tossed down a rock, which Alfucius caught with a perfect roll, only for it to glow red: in four rounds it would explode on whoever touched it last. "I don't like spice. I'm very vanilla," he protested. As if in answer, it began to snow, the snow tasted of vanilla, and the lava cooled into what the Arbiter called "a snowy vanilla lava."

### Blinky's Sacrifice

Blinky landed on Gerald's shadowy hand. "What the fuck is this?" asked the box. "His name is Blinky! I literally couldn't give two shits about him! Take him!" Alfucius shouted. "Bottoms up then," said Gerald, and swallowed him. Blinky's light flickered out inside the shadows like a dying bulb. "He may have been a homunculus," the Grandmaster said, "but you are the real monster." Alfucius wondered aloud whether they were the bad guys.

The box rumbled and flipped open, and a book with Blinky's wings fluttered out: **a tome of understanding**, which made its reader cleverer. "Does that mean that Gerald shit out a book with Blinky's wings?" the Grandmaster asked. The Arbiter couldn't speak to the biology of shadow creatures, "but yes, he shit out a book." Alfucius wiped the leftover shadow feces off the cover and cried, "Oh, Blinky, I never doubted you for a second!"

Freshly brilliant, he chugged coffee from a container in his robe and threw down an Eldritch Cannon, a clockwork gun on four mechanical spider legs. He kicked the hot rock to Dolly, who caught it, then ran to hide behind the floating dragon skull. The cannon just stood there menacingly.

### Time Out Behind the Skull

A voice filled his head: Coen, telepathically chanting "Never gonna give you up." She dashed to his side, conjured a bonfire and patted the ground beside her. He sat down. They checked the stands: the Arbiter had his feet up, picking his teeth with a knife, and didn't care. The Grandmaster saw everything but kept her mouth shut.

Gadgets tumbled out of Alfucius's sleeves, and he came clean. "I don't know any magic at all. I just have these. I have a gun!" He admitted he had planned to shoot her in the face. He tried a sob story about rescuing his son's soul, then dropped it at once: "There's a ring of like three wishes, and I really, really want it." He pitched killing her and promising to wish her back. He had fallen on hard times, he confessed, and had spent most of his money on the commute. Blinky, he added, had been made of a diamond, "so in retrospect, I probably shouldn't have fed him to a demon." Aragorl changed again, her fur turning black-and-white spotted, and Coen offered him friends of his own: "I could give you lots of friends." "You mean those freaks?" Coen, unswayed, made clear she wanted the wishes for herself.

### The Grandmaster Demands Blood

The Grandmaster's laugh cut through their plotting. The grass of these fields, she told them, grows not from water but from blood. "So I require a death." She asked whether the Arbiter was thinking what she was thinking. "That I'd really like to just go home," he replied. She made his chains heavier with a spell, and he raised a half-hearted hand. Mist gathered over the dragon bones, and **a ghost dragon** rose with a roar. "I'm sorry, that's what you think a dragon sounds like?" "Trust me, it's a ghost dragon. It's different."

"I hope you know I blame you for this," Alfucius told Coen, and he tried to bolt. She grabbed his ankle. The Acolytes' faith surged into both champions like a whole can of caffeinated soda. Alfucius reached for his gun to shoot her and fumbled his bullets onto the ground. Coen used her surge to calm his anger through her familiar, and they greeted each other anew: "There's a dragon behind us. Probably should take care of that."

### Friendship, Ballet and a Bullet

The dragon breathed a necrotic mist that dried them out. Alfucius took the full blast and wiped his tears with his beard: "Alfucius has a brand and he's keeping it up." Its ghostly tail swept through Coen and dropped her: "It's not you, it's me." Aragorl threw the hot rock, which passed straight through the dragon and reappeared in her own pocket.

Alfucius fired scorching rays at the dragon, but his cannon hadn't joined the friendship. It blasted Aragorl, who was immune to fire and lost only the white of her head tuft. "My cannon's getting a little bit jealous." Coen then summoned **a twenty-foot greater demon in Aragorl's image**, with crab claws, spindly legs, a tutu and pink Barbie stilettos. "All legs, baby… Honestly, she's a leg model." Beaming like a mom at a spelling bee, Coen spelled out "Dragon, D-A-R-G-U-N. Dragon," and from then on it was "the dragoon." Little Aragorl threw the rock again, and this time the dragon caught it, baffled, while Big Aragorl pirouetted and kicked it twice in the face. The Acolytes threw roses. Then the dragon clawed Coen with the rock still in its grip, and the fourth round arrived: it exploded.

The Grandmaster blessed them both and demanded massive damage. Alfucius shot the dragon, then fired again with a perfect roll. **The bullet carved a tunnel through its chest**, and the ghost burst into green, purple and pink fairy lights that drifted away on the wind.

### Blood, Tulips and One Unlucky Goblin

"I don't think that was blood," Coen observed. "That was light." They agreed on a small sacrifice and moved to stab each other at the same moment. Invisible Aragorl beat them to it and gnawed Alfucius's arm until blood dripped onto the grass. "Well… that will do," said the Grandmaster. Yellow tulips with tiny teeth sprang up where it fell. One snapped at Alfucius, and Coen snapped back, biting off their heads. Both Aragorls joined in, tearing up dirt and worms in a shower of yellow petals.

The Grandmaster granted both champions their money and their wishes, though she kept her distance from the giant doll, whose every blink was a thunderclap. The Arbiter was furious: "You know that I love when the deathmatches don't end in death." His chains grew heavier still. So he flicked a finger and sent **a goblin from the stands** flying miles into the sky, where it crashed into the Acolyte Das Fuster. Coen called after it in Goblin: "Goodbye."

"Isn't death just the friends we find along the way?" Alfucius mused, putting an arm around Coen. She removed it and set little Aragorl on his shoulder instead. [[Goblins]] walked the aisles tossing stuffed Aragorls to the crowd, and one plush's leg stabbed somebody in the eye.
