Two dicks, one team, and nobody in charge. In the second part of Tip to Tip, **[[Richard Knottley]]** and **[[Richard S. Skelman]]** returned to the graveyard of [[The Midnight Mausoleum]] to fight to the death for the leadership of Team B. Over the course of the fight Knottley lost an arm, then all of his flesh, then his body, then his mustache, and finally his grip on reality. He ascended into the cosmos still rolling natural 20s, and the skeleton who had spent thousands of years trying to ascend was left standing, very confused, as the new manager.

## The Story

### Chinchillas on the Loose

Deep below [[The Dread Fortress]], the halls filled with yips and yelps. A tiny pink imp, [[Thea]], was chasing a horde of [[Chinchillas|chinchillas]] under furniture and through doorways, in every direction except toward the arena. When [[The Arbiter]] asked how this had happened, Thea explained that the chinchillas "went poo poo," so she had changed their cage. She had opened the door and forgotten to set up the tube to the other cage. She also hadn't shut the door, because "doors are heavy here… a really poor design choice," and her magic is "cute magic that goes poof," not door magic.

The Arbiter shot a bolt from his finger and slammed the door shut, then opened it and shut it again, over and over. "Can I have that power?" Thea asked. "You have that power! It's a basic thing!" Thea tried a little shock of her own and was amazed. She decided to save it for the returning members of Team B. She also had a plan to give the night's winner a chinchilla as a welcoming gift. The Arbiter approved, since "the less of them in the Dread Fortress, the better," and asked her to keep it between them.

Then Thea, who had been "reviewing my notes," asked when the Arbiter had ever brought the champions before bringing the Grandmaster. He said he wanted to try something new. "Oh, I should have read your mind. Damn it," she said, and ran her head into the door several times. The Arbiter suggested "two, three more times," then gave up: "You already ruined it."

[[Grandmaster Elusia Skypiercer|The Grandmaster]] was right on the other side of that door. She stepped in, lifting her skirt to look at the beasts scurrying underfoot. The Arbiter assured her the chinchillas were exercising, for "cheaper healthcare," and when she asked where the champions were, he clapped his hands in frustration and ripped open a portal. Through it walked Richard Knottley and Richard S. Skelman. "Oh, so the dicks, pair of dicks are back," said the Grandmaster, who claimed that the Arbiter had unwrapped a pair of dicks for his last birthday too.

### A Deathmatch for Management

Tonight was no mission, the Grandmaster announced. Last week the team had proven they could at least find Thea, but they still had no name and no hierarchy. As the Arbiter put it, "the only way that we know how to solve that problem is to have you fight to the death." Thea reminded everyone that it wasn't permanent.

Talk of dominance hierarchies led to an existential detour. Knottley asked whether Thea was "like a pink and little like wormy like kind of thing," and Thea insisted she was people, because [[The Acolytes]] had said so. Knottley allowed that there was a hashtag, "so it has to be real," but warned that hashtags had also turned the Justice League into a four-hour disaster. How would she know if she was people? "That's a journey you're going to have to go on," said Knottley. "Far, far away from here," added Skelman.

The Grandmaster opened a portal to the mausoleum they had visited before. "Grandfather, hello again," said Knottley, greeting the grave of Skelman's grandfather, whom the team had killed again last time. The Grandmaster was more upset that neither champion was wearing the team t-shirts she had given them. Skelman's was tight around the ribs, and Knottley had been given a small when he "very clearly needed a 2X." "It's always a management problem," said Skelman.

The prize was laid out: the leader would name the team, lead it, file its complaints, control its budget and maybe get a chinchilla. Knottley explained that chinchillas run the government. To prove it, the Grandmaster clapped and twelve chinchillas paraded out, each wearing a fake mustache. "It's the Parliament!" cried Skelman. "Our government is being run right before our eyes." For their good behavior they were given the front row of the floating grandstands, and the Acolytes who usually sat there were pushed back a couple of rows. Complaints, the Arbiter explained, went into his filing system, which was a trash can. The budget, which he measured with his hands, was "about this." Knottley protested that they were worth more, "especially when we have our friend with us, the third dick," meaning their absent teammate [[Killy Yesman]]. The Grandmaster agreed that for a good show they could probably double it, then flew up to sit with the chinchillas.

The Arbiter spread his white wings and rose above the crowd: "Acolytes! Are you ready for some blood?" Skelman pointed out that he didn't have any.

### The Dick Twist

Skelman seemed frozen in the moment, and Knottley took the chance to tell him, kindly, "You might be pale and dead, but deep down inside, there's something beautiful in there." Then he opened with his signature move, **the Dick Twist: a dance-off**, best of three.

Knottley rolled a natural 20, striking classic Vogue poses as cameras flashed ("It's a grower, not a shower"). Skelman's hip popped out. Knottley rolled a second natural 20, all bouncing elbows over literal graves, and Skelman's knee popped out. Skelman had thought this was a battle to the death, and Thea said it looked more like a high school prom. Skelman confessed he never went to his: "Nobody asked me… If I had tear ducts, there would be tears." Thea dropped to one knee and asked him to prom. Skelman, worried about the legal trouble of an old dead man dating "a small yet somewhat tiny child-esque creature," hesitated, but Thea only wanted to help him dance. She clicked a button, a disco ball dropped from nowhere, and the tombstones lit up in colors.

The Arbiter had already declared Knottley the winner, but the Grandmaster insisted on a third dance for fun. Skelman lost that one too. One of his arms fell off, Thea stepped on his head mid-move, and she limped away crushed: "My first prom was ruined."

Riding high, Knottley announced that he was here to "break boundaries… and I uncover secret truths." He warned Skelman that he was about to physically assault him because people had found out about the woman he buried ("I've buried so many…"), then launched into the air and brought his elbow down. Skelman's skull spun around comically, and the two traded a blind slap fest until it ran out of steam.

### Bones, Peloton and a Ghost

Skelman answered with a challenge of his own. He calmly removed his own arm at the shoulder and looked at Knottley expectantly. Knottley could not match it, so Thea helped. She tugged, then used her teeth, then strapped on a rocket pack, pull-started it like a lawnmower, and on the third pull blasted off with Knottley's left arm. She waved goodbye with it and, at Knottley's suggestion, tossed it into the crowd like a baseball, where it karate-chopped the Acolyte Cookie Monster Steve in the forehead.

Skelman threw the zombie arm he had picked up last time, which tickled Knottley into fits of laughter. Then he donned a leather jacket and hat and delivered a pointy-booted biker kick that Knottley met so perfectly that neither got anywhere, and both wore themselves out. Then something wonderful happened. "Skelly, your spirit is bonding with mine," said Knottley, and the bones of his lost arm flew out of the crowd "like little Iron Man pieces" and locked into place. "That is the coolest fucking thing I've ever seen," said Skelman, who has been a skeleton for a millennium. He celebrated with a front-flipping leg drop into Knottley's sternum.

Knottley tested his new powers by firing his bony fist at Skelman. It veered off course, corrected itself at the last second and burst into bone shrapnel, costing Skelman some ribs. Knottley shook off the tickling by smashing the zombie hand against the ground, which Skelman noted might be his ex-girlfriend's. "We never really resolved that plot line last time."

Then Knottley revealed a secret. Through his "connection with the company Peloton," he had stolen a prototype arc bike reactor from their factory: a small orb pulsing with radiation. Thea held it while he fixed his belt, and her fingernails melted off ("I love it when my face melts"). She dribbled it like a basketball, building power with each bounce, and slammed it into his chest. **Knottley's flesh melted away.** He was now a skeleton coated in melted Peloton, with the glowing core as his heart. "And now I'm powered by Peloton."

Skelman demanded a durability test. He took the stance he had imagined playing Captain Falcon back in his N64 days, "back when I had flesh and bone," and landed a "Falcon, punch!" right on the core. Knottley collapsed into a pile of bones. Skelman explained why he himself never fell apart: Francis, the little gelatinous slime that holds him together. "I get a new one of these, I think, about every week." He tried to build Knottley into a handbasket so they could go to hell in one, but the pieces were wrong ("You put my hands where my feet went"). His attempt to drop the bones down a well failed too.

Thea offered encouragement ("Remember, you're more than just bones. You have mental powers") and some chewing gum to stick himself back together. Both champions declined the gum. It was Skelman who reached him in the end, sitting cross-legged by his skull for a pep talk: being a skeleton "is really just a force of will," and "you can be anything you want to be." Then he noogied the skull. The Acolytes chanted "Be who you wanna be!" and Knottley took it to heart. His bones turned to dust, swirled, and re-formed as **the ghostly specter of Richard Knottley**.

When Thea tried to call the round herself, the Arbiter reminded her that he and the Grandmaster were the referees and she just dealt with the cards. He called a break right as she freed the next card, and Thea broke it over her knee. "Grandmaster, remind me to send her to the therapist."

### Into the Box

After the break, the Grandmaster was raving about the trailer for *Bessie and the Cowdettes*, bovine astronauts versus evil alien space turkeys, "my top movie of 2024." ("Their studio is really milking it," said the Arbiter.) The Acolytes chanted for blood, and the movie's promotion swallowed the arena. The lights dimmed, the ground turned sticky, a PBR appeared in every hand, even Knottley's ghostly one, and a mechanical bull rose beneath both champions. "Roll de yee-haw, motherfuckers," said the Grandmaster. Knottley shrugged off the ride. Skelman got thrown around.

Bored, the Arbiter slammed his hands together. [[The Box of Chaos]] fell from the sky like a rocket and, "because of technical reasons," hovered in midair as a column of yellow and pink fire shot from its open lid. Knottley, whose ghostly memory was already slipping, picked Thea up, told her to hold her nose for a cannonball and tossed her in. Shadowy hands sprang out: "Hey, what the fuck is going on? … You can't just throw shit in my box." After Thea was set down gently instead, she made herself at home ("Hi, I live here now… You have a lot of toilet paper"). The box's shadowy tenant demanded rent, so she knocked out four of her teeth on the box's edge. "What a sucker," he said, and Knottley received a screaming vial of black liquid that soaked into his ghost and scrambled his fighting instincts.

Knottley then lifted Thea out and had her pitch all her droppings at Skelman. "It's my gift to you! I love you, Skelvin!" she cried, as poop splattered him like a machine gun. "I'm used to the goo, but not the poo," said Skelman. Knottley flew into Skelman to clean him off and possessed his bones. Skelman delivered a lecture on how possession is "inherently evil," like charm spells, before trying to shake hands and passing straight through him. Ghosts lose their memories, he warned.

Then Skelman produced a secret weapon. An Acolyte backstage named Luigi, with a great mustache and a brother named Mario, had told him ghosts are weak to vacuum cleaners and handed him one. Skelman pull-started it and sucked Knottley inside. "Now I have a ghost in a box," he said. "You have a dick in a box," said the Arbiter.

Skelman offered the vacuum, ghost included, to the Box of Chaos and asked for new cosmic dice, since his had been terrible all night. A magic blue pill appeared and was snatched back ("That's mine. Sorry"). Instead he got the **[[Shield of the Golden King]]**, which the Arbiter promptly took back, pulled on a welding mask, and blowtorched into a set of golden dice. As for the vacuum, "I didn't really want this guy. I just wanted the vacuum. This place is a mess." The Arbiter reversed it and blew Knottley out into the ether.

### The Bone Zone

As the crowd chanted "Bone zone!", Skelman expanded his consciousness and turned the world into his danger dungeon. The only thing that could hurt Knottley more than a wrong conspiracy theory was himself, so Skelman brought in the statue the team had met the week before: a winged likeness of Knottley wearing a mustache "with a nice thick layer of adhesive." Was he subcontracting the Deathmatch? "I own the dungeon. I subcontract the work inside that dungeon." The Grandmaster called it a sign of leadership. The statue opened a pit trap, but Knottley held firm and it became a two-inch drop that only brought them closer. "It's a meet cute," said the Grandmaster. "I want you inside of me," said the statue, and then the dungeon faded.

Thea found Skelman despondent. "This dick has bested me by not even trying… He just seems to continue falling upwards," he said. "I've been this way for thousands of years and yet he ascends immediately." Thea told him being different didn't make Knottley better, and that bones were the real winner. "Great words, awful timing," said Skelman, still covered in her poop. Then the Chinchilla Parliament pulled out translators and heckled both champions with painfully accurate insults ("Why can't you even roll a die?"), wounding them more than either had managed all night. "Why is our government so mean?" Skelman asked.

Knottley, longing for his statue, simply stood still and waited. Skelman gathered all he had left and sent him back into the danger dungeon, where the statue waited at the end of a long hallway of traps that Skelman's lackeys had coated in ectoplasmic goo. "Also, lots of fire. So much fire." An axe came down, a spike box pinned the ghost, and flamethrowers cooked him "like a marshmallow." The wall of lightning, though, he simply sidestepped: "I sidestep court workers all the time when they're trying to serve me papers."

### Ascension

Battered and nearly spent, Knottley reached the end of the shaft. "Statue, I say we become one." The statue admired his thick Peloton calves and varicose veins ("I'm a vein guy. Always have been"), opened its angel wings, and Knottley's ghost merged into it. When Skelman objected that the statue was on his payroll, it reminded him that it was allowed a fifteen-minute break.

The statue's rock fist landed, and Skelman's golden dice shattered on the very first block. Resigned, he told Knottley to finish it: "Become the manager you were meant to be!" Knottley stacked everything into one blow ("Status me, Dad!"), but Francis shot out a pseudopod and shoved the statue back. "I had accepted my defeat. But my slimy friend had not." Knottley raised his arms, the clouds parted, and a swarm of Peloton bikes swirled through the sky and blasted them both with lightning. "I hate cardio!" Skelman screamed. He swung back with everything he had and landed nothing. Knottley even healed. "He's healing and I'm dying!"

But Knottley had won too hard. He had pushed himself past his limit, and he began to fade. "You burned yourself to burn him!" cried Thea. **Knottley became one with the universe**, all around them, his voice drifting down: "We've been looking for you to talk to you about your car's extended warranty." Skelman, who had spent thousands of years trying to ascend, was outraged: "You pushed yourself too hard, too fast."

### Team D

The Grandmaster came down from the chinchillas' row. Luck is part of winning, she told Skelman: "Don't ever underestimate it. Leverage it." He allowed that perhaps he was management material, and she named him leader of Team B. His first act was the name. "We three dicks came together as one to do what people thought was impossible, find Thea, clearly the most chaotic object in the universe… I think Team D is here to stay." Thea clarified: "For dicks." The ascended Knottley chimed in from the cosmos: "Team D, four. Also, taxation is theft."

The Grandmaster assured him Knottley would return in his fleshy state next time, because ascension is a loop. "It's not a loop, it's a spiral," said the universe. Skelman began asking who and what he was, and the Grandmaster, who just runs the Dread Fortress and doesn't want to be anyone's spiritual guide, asked for somebody to come get him. The Arbiter escorted him off, admitting he'd hoped for the name "Dick Dynasty" and promising to see about getting his bones massaged. The Grandmaster closed by telling the Acolytes they had witnessed the birth of the second team, and that "death is not the ending, and it is not what we should fear."

## Dried Blood

In the after-show, the new team lead laid out his agenda. His first order of business was getting their third dick out, since "he's in too deep." His second was to stop paying taxes on his many dungeons, because he is now, as Richard taught him, "a sovereign citizen." The Grandmaster didn't want to say no, so she allowed it. Knottley, soon to return to human form, complained that "it just won't let me die." He was told that the tinfoil hat he kept putting on could be surgically implanted on the inside ("They call me hammerhead"). Skelman promised the Acolytes that Team D would find their third dick and "create the largest dickazord that anyone has ever seen." At the table, the Arbiter's player told Knottley's that he only lost because he won too hard.
